Mensuration

Learn how to calculate lengths, perimeters, areas, surface areas and volumes of common geometric shapes.

1. Measurement

Measurement is the process of finding the size or amount of something. In mensuration, we commonly measure length, perimeter, area and volume.

Common Units

  • Length: millimetres (mm), centimetres (cm), metres (m), kilometres (km)
  • Area: cm², m², km²
  • Volume: cm³, m³

Important Idea

The unit you use must match what you are measuring. A length uses units such as metres, while an area uses square metres and volume uses cubic metres.

Example
A table has a length of 2 m.

Its measurement is simply:
2 metres

Since we are measuring only length, we use metres, not square metres or cubic metres.

2. Perimeter

The perimeter is the total distance around the outside of a two-dimensional shape.

To find the perimeter, add all the sides of the shape.

Perimeter = sum of all sides

Rectangle

P = 2(l + w)
Example
A rectangle has a length of 8 cm and a width of 5 cm.

P = 2(l + w)
P = 2(8 + 5)
P = 2 × 13
P = 26 cm

Square

P = 4s
Example
A square has a side length of 6 cm.

P = 4 × 6
P = 24 cm

3. Area

Area is the amount of space covered by a two-dimensional shape.

Area is measured in square units such as cm² and m².

Rectangle

Area = length × width

A = lw
Example
A rectangle has a length of 10 cm and a width of 4 cm.

A = 10 × 4
A = 40 cm²

Square

A = s²
Example
A square has a side of 7 cm.

A = 7²
A = 49 cm²

4. Area of Triangles

A triangle has three sides. To find its area, multiply the base by the perpendicular height and divide the result by two.

A = ½bh

Here, b represents the base and h represents the perpendicular height.

Example
A triangle has a base of 12 cm and a height of 5 cm.

A = ½ × 12 × 5
A = 6 × 5
A = 30 cm²

5. Circles

A circle is a curved shape in which every point on the circumference is the same distance from the centre.

Important Terms

  • Radius: distance from the centre to the circumference.
  • Diameter: distance across the circle through the centre.
  • Circumference: distance around the circle.
d = 2r

C = 2πr

C = πd

Area of a Circle

A = πr²
Example
A circle has a radius of 7 cm.

A = πr²
A = π × 7²
A = 49π
Using π ≈ 22/7:
A = 49 × 22/7
A = 154 cm²

6. Surface Area

Surface area is the total area of all the surfaces of a three-dimensional object.

Cuboid

SA = 2(lw + lh + wh)
Example
A cuboid has:
l = 5 cm
w = 3 cm
h = 2 cm

SA = 2(5×3 + 5×2 + 3×2)
SA = 2(15 + 10 + 6)
SA = 2(31)
SA = 62 cm²

7. Volume

Volume is the amount of space occupied by a three-dimensional object.

Volume is measured in cubic units such as cm³ and m³.

Cuboid

V = lwh
Example
A cuboid has a length of 8 cm, width of 4 cm and height of 3 cm.

V = 8 × 4 × 3
V = 32 × 3
V = 96 cm³

Cube

V = s³
Example
A cube has a side length of 5 cm.

V = 5³
V = 5 × 5 × 5
V = 125 cm³

8. Composite Shapes

A composite shape is a shape made by combining two or more simpler shapes.

To find its area, divide the shape into familiar shapes, calculate each area, and then add or subtract as required.

Example
Suppose a shape can be divided into a rectangle and a triangle.

Rectangle area = 40 cm²
Triangle area = 15 cm²

Total area:
40 + 15
Total area = 55 cm²

9. Unit Conversion

Sometimes a measurement must be converted from one unit to another before solving a problem.

Common Length Conversions

  • 1 m = 100 cm
  • 1 cm = 10 mm
  • 1 km = 1000 m

Example

Convert 3 metres to centimetres
1 m = 100 cm

Therefore:
3 × 100 = 300

3 m = 300 cm
