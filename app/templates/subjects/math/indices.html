Indices and Logarithms

Indices and logarithms are closely connected ideas used throughout algebra, equations, science, finance and higher mathematics. Indices describe repeated multiplication, while logarithms help us find unknown powers.

Indices Basics Laws of Indices Zero & Negative Fractional Indices Standard Form Simplification Index Equations Logarithms Log Laws Change of Base Log Equations Exponential Equations Applications

1. Understanding Indices

An index, also called an exponent or power, tells us how many times a number is multiplied by itself.

aⁿ = a × a × a × ... × a

The number a is called the base, while n is called the index or exponent.

Example

Example:

Expand 5³.

5³ = 5 × 5 × 5
= 125

Important Examples

2² = 4
3³ = 27
10² = 100
10³ = 1000

Indices allow repeated multiplication to be written compactly and are especially useful when expressions become complicated.

2. Laws of Indices

The laws of indices allow us to simplify expressions involving powers.

Multiplication of Powers with the Same Base

When powers with the same base are multiplied, add their indices.

aᵐ × aⁿ = aᵐ⁺ⁿ
Example:
2³ × 2⁴
= 2³⁺⁴
= 2⁷
= 128

Division of Powers with the Same Base

aᵐ ÷ aⁿ = aᵐ⁻ⁿ
Example:
5⁶ ÷ 5²
= 5⁶⁻²
= 5⁴

Power Raised to Another Power

(aᵐ)ⁿ = aᵐⁿ
Example:
(2³)²
= 2⁶
= 64

Power of a Product

(ab)ⁿ = aⁿbⁿ
Example:
(2 × 3)²
= 2² × 3²
= 4 × 9
= 36

Power of a Quotient

(a/b)ⁿ = aⁿ/bⁿ

3. Zero and Negative Indices

Zero Index

Any non-zero number raised to the power zero equals 1.

a⁰ = 1
Example:
7⁰ = 1
100⁰ = 1
x⁰ = 1

Negative Indices

A negative index means that the base is moved to the denominator and the sign of the index becomes positive.

a⁻ⁿ = 1/aⁿ
Example:
2⁻³
= 1/2³
= 1/8

Another Example

x⁻⁴ = 1/x⁴

Negative indices do not mean that the final answer must be negative.

4. Fractional Indices

Fractional indices are connected to roots.

a¹⁄ⁿ = ⁿ√a

Therefore, an index of 1/2 represents a square root, while an index of 1/3 represents a cube root.

a¹⁄² = √a
a¹⁄³ = ∛a

General Rule

aᵐ⁄ⁿ = ⁿ√(aᵐ)
Example:
16¹⁄²
= √16
= 4
Example:
27²⁄³
= (∛27)²
= 3²
= 9

Negative Fractional Indices

a⁻ᵐ⁄ⁿ = 1 / ⁿ√(aᵐ)

5. Standard Form and Powers of Ten

Standard form is used to write very large or very small numbers conveniently.

A × 10ⁿ

For standard form, A must be at least 1 but less than 10.

Example:

Write 4500000 in standard form.

4.5 × 10⁶
Example:

Write 0.00032 in standard form.

3.2 × 10⁻⁴

Multiplication in Standard Form

(a × 10ᵐ)(b × 10ⁿ) = ab × 10ᵐ⁺ⁿ

Division in Standard Form

(a × 10ᵐ)/(b × 10ⁿ) = (a/b) × 10ᵐ⁻ⁿ

6. Simplifying Expressions with Indices

When simplifying expressions containing indices, first identify terms with the same base and then apply the appropriate law.

Example:

Simplify:

x³ × x⁵
= x⁸
Example:

Simplify:

a⁷ ÷ a²
= a⁵
Example:

Simplify:

(x²y³)²
= x⁴y⁶

Important Tip

Do not add indices unless the terms are being multiplied with the same base. Do not multiply indices unless one power is raised to another power.

7. Equations Involving Indices

Some equations contain the unknown in the index. These are called exponential equations.

Example:
2ˣ = 8

Since 8 = 2³:

2ˣ = 2³
x = 3
Example:
3ˣ = 81

Since 81 = 3⁴:

3ˣ = 3⁴
x = 4

When the two sides can be expressed using the same base, compare their indices.

8. Introduction to Logarithms

A logarithm answers the question: "What power must this base be raised to in order to obtain this number?"

logₐ x = y ⇔ aʸ = x
Example:
log₂ 8 = 3

because:

2³ = 8

Another Example

log₁₀ 1000 = 3
because
10³ = 1000

Parts of a Logarithm

logₐ x = y

a is the base, x is the argument, and y is the logarithm.

Common Logarithm

A logarithm with base 10 is called a common logarithm.

log₁₀ x

Natural Logarithm

A logarithm with base e is called the natural logarithm. It is written as ln.

ln x = logₑ x

9. Laws of Logarithms

Product Law

logₐ(MN) = logₐM + logₐN
Example:
log₂(8 × 4)
= log₂8 + log₂4
= 3 + 2
= 5

Quotient Law

logₐ(M/N) = logₐM - logₐN

Power Law

logₐ(Mⁿ) = n logₐM
Example:
log₂(8²)
= 2log₂8
= 2 × 3
= 6

Logarithm of One

logₐ1 = 0

Logarithm of the Base

logₐa = 1

10. Change of Base

The change-of-base formula allows a logarithm to be converted to another base.

logₐb = logₓb / logₓa

The most common choices for x are 10 and e.

logₐb = log b / log a
Example:

Express log₂10 using common logarithms.

log₂10 = log10 / log2

11. Solving Logarithmic Equations

Logarithmic equations can often be solved by converting logarithmic form into exponential form or by applying the laws of logarithms.

Example:
log₂x = 5

Convert to exponential form:

2⁵ = x
x = 32

Example with a Product

Question:
log₂x + log₂4 = 5

Use the product law:

log₂(4x) = 5

Convert to exponential form:

4x = 2⁵
4x = 32
x = 8

Important Restriction

The argument of a real logarithm must be positive.

x > 0

12. Exponential Equations and Logarithms

Logarithms are especially useful when the unknown appears in an exponent and the two sides cannot easily be written using the same base.

Example:
2ˣ = 7

Take logarithms of both sides:

log(2ˣ) = log7

Apply the power law:

x log2 = log7

Therefore:

x = log7 / log2

General Exponential Form

aˣ = b

x = logₐb

This relationship is one of the main reasons logarithms are useful in mathematics.

13. Applications of Indices and Logarithms

Indices and logarithms are not only algebraic topics. They are used to model real-world situations involving growth, decay, repeated multiplication and extremely large or small quantities.

Compound Growth

Compound growth can be represented using powers.

A = P(1 + r)ⁿ

where P is the initial amount, r is the growth rate per period and n is the number of periods.

Compound Interest

A = P(1 + r/n)ⁿᵗ

This formula is used when interest is compounded multiple times per year.

Exponential Decay

Quantities such as radioactive substances, some populations and certain physical processes can decrease exponentially.

A = A₀(1-r)ᵗ

Finding Time Using Logarithms

If time appears as an exponent, logarithms can be used to isolate it.

Example:

Suppose:

100(1.05)ᵗ = 200

Divide by 100:

1.05ᵗ = 2

Take logarithms:

t log(1.05) = log2

Therefore:

t = log2 / log(1.05)
