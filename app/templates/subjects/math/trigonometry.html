1. Trigonometry Basics

Trigonometry is the branch of mathematics concerned with relationships between angles and sides of triangles.

It can be used to find unknown lengths and angles when enough information about a triangle is known.

Uses of Trigonometry

  • Finding unknown sides.
  • Finding unknown angles.
  • Finding heights and distances.
  • Navigation and surveying.
  • Engineering and construction.
  • Physics and wave motion.

2. Angles

An angle represents the amount of rotation between two rays that meet at a common point.

Types of Angles

  • Acute: greater than 0° and less than 90°.
  • Right: exactly 90°.
  • Obtuse: greater than 90° but less than 180°.
  • Straight: exactly 180°.
  • Reflex: greater than 180° but less than 360°.

Degrees and Radians

Trigonometric angles can be measured in degrees or radians.

180° = π radians

360° = 2π radians
Example

Convert 60° to radians.

60 × π/180
= π/3 radians

3. Right-Angled Triangles

A right-angled triangle has one angle equal to 90°. The side opposite the right angle is the hypotenuse.

Pythagorean Theorem

a² + b² = c²

The theorem allows us to calculate an unknown side when two sides of a right-angled triangle are known.

Example

A right-angled triangle has shorter sides of 6 cm and 8 cm. Find the hypotenuse.

c² = 6² + 8²
c² = 36 + 64
c² = 100
c = 10 cm

4. Trigonometric Ratios

The three basic trigonometric ratios for right-angled triangles are sine, cosine and tangent.

SOH-CAH-TOA

sin θ = opposite / hypotenuse

cos θ = adjacent / hypotenuse

tan θ = opposite / adjacent

SOH-CAH-TOA is a useful way of remembering which sides belong to each ratio.

Finding an Unknown Side

Example

A right triangle has a 30° angle and a hypotenuse of 10 cm. Find the opposite side.

sin 30° = opposite / 10
0.5 = opposite / 10
opposite = 5 cm

Inverse Trigonometric Functions

θ = sin⁻¹(opposite / hypotenuse)

θ = cos⁻¹(adjacent / hypotenuse)

θ = tan⁻¹(opposite / adjacent)

5. Special Angles

The angles 0°, 30°, 45°, 60° and 90° occur frequently in trigonometry and have exact values.

Sine

sin 0° = 0
sin 30° = 1/2
sin 45° = √2/2
sin 60° = √3/2
sin 90° = 1

Cosine

cos 0° = 1
cos 30° = √3/2
cos 45° = √2/2
cos 60° = 1/2
cos 90° = 0

Tangent

tan 0° = 0
tan 30° = √3/3
tan 45° = 1
tan 60° = √3

6. Unit Circle

The unit circle is a circle with radius 1 centered at the origin of the coordinate plane.

It provides a way to define trigonometric functions for angles beyond the basic acute angles of a right-angled triangle.

Coordinates

(x, y) = (cos θ, sin θ)

The x-coordinate represents cos θ and the y-coordinate represents sin θ.

Signs in the Four Quadrants

  • Quadrant I: all three are positive.
  • Quadrant II: sine is positive.
  • Quadrant III: tangent is positive.
  • Quadrant IV: cosine is positive.

7. Trigonometric Identities

A trigonometric identity is an equation that remains true for every value for which both sides are defined.

Pythagorean Identity

sin²θ + cos²θ = 1

Other Pythagorean Identities

1 + tan²θ = sec²θ

1 + cot²θ = csc²θ

Reciprocal Identities

csc θ = 1 / sin θ
sec θ = 1 / cos θ
cot θ = 1 / tan θ

Quotient Identities

tan θ = sin θ / cos θ
cot θ = cos θ / sin θ
Example

If sin θ = 3/5 and θ is acute, find cos θ.

sin²θ + cos²θ = 1
(3/5)² + cos²θ = 1
9/25 + cos²θ = 1
cos²θ = 16/25
cos θ = 4/5

8. Trigonometric Equations

Trigonometric equations contain trigonometric functions and require us to find the values of an unknown angle.

Example

Solve sin θ = 1/2 for 0° ≤ θ ≤ 360°.

sin⁻¹(1/2) = 30°

Sine is positive in Quadrants I and II.

θ = 30° or 150°

Because trigonometric functions are periodic, an equation can have several solutions within a given interval.

9. Trigonometric Graphs

Sine, cosine and tangent are periodic functions. Their graphs repeat at regular intervals.

Sine

y = sin x

The sine function has a maximum value of 1 and a minimum value of -1.

Cosine

y = cos x

The cosine function also ranges from -1 to 1.

Tangent

y = tan x

Tangent has vertical asymptotes where cosine is zero and has a period of 180° or π radians.

General Sine Function

y = A sin(Bx + C) + D

The constants can change the amplitude, period, horizontal position and vertical position of the graph.

10. Non-Right-Angled Triangles

Trigonometry can also be used with triangles that do not contain a right angle.

Sine Rule

a / sin A = b / sin B = c / sin C

Cosine Rule

a² = b² + c² - 2bc cos A

Area of a Triangle

Area = 1/2 ab sin C
Example

Two sides of a triangle are 8 cm and 10 cm and the included angle is 60°. Find its area.

Area = 1/2 × 8 × 10 × sin 60°

Area = 40 × √3/2

Area = 20√3 cm²

11. Applications of Trigonometry

Trigonometry can be used to solve real-world problems involving heights, distances, angles and directions.

Angle of Elevation

The angle of elevation is measured upward from a horizontal line to an object above the observer.

Angle of Depression

The angle of depression is measured downward from a horizontal line to an object below the observer.

Example

A person stands 20 m from a building and observes the top at an angle of elevation of 40°. Find the height of the building, ignoring the person's height.

tan 40° = height / 20

height = 20 tan 40°

height ≈ 16.78 m

Applications

  • Surveying.
  • Navigation.
  • Engineering.
  • Construction.
  • Physics.
  • Astronomy.
  • Computer graphics.
