Geometry

Learn the basic ideas of shapes, lines, angles, measurements, areas, volumes, transformations and other important geometry concepts used in secondary school mathematics.

1. Geometry Basics

Geometry is the branch of mathematics that deals with shapes, sizes, positions, distances and properties of objects.

Point

A point represents an exact position. It has no length, width or thickness.

Line

A line is a straight path that extends infinitely in both directions.

Line Segment

A line segment is part of a line with two definite endpoints.

Ray

A ray has one endpoint and extends infinitely in one direction.

2. Lines and Their Relationships

Parallel Lines

Parallel lines are lines that remain the same distance apart and never meet.

Perpendicular Lines

Perpendicular lines intersect at a right angle of 90°.

Intersecting Lines

Intersecting lines are lines that cross at a point.

Transversal

A transversal is a line that crosses two or more other lines. When parallel lines are crossed by a transversal, several useful angle relationships are formed.

3. Angles

An angle is formed when two rays meet at a common endpoint. The common endpoint is called the vertex.

Types of Angles

  • Acute angle: less than 90°
  • Right angle: exactly 90°
  • Obtuse angle: greater than 90° but less than 180°
  • Straight angle: exactly 180°
  • Reflex angle: greater than 180° but less than 360°
  • Full angle: exactly 360°

Complementary Angles

Two angles are complementary when their sum is 90°.

A + B = 90°

Supplementary Angles

Two angles are supplementary when their sum is 180°.

A + B = 180°

Angles on a Straight Line

Angles on a straight line add up to 180°.

Angles Around a Point

Angles around a point add up to 360°.

4. Triangles

A triangle is a polygon with three sides, three vertices and three interior angles.

Triangle Angle Sum

The interior angles of every triangle add up to 180°.

A + B + C = 180°

Types by Sides

  • Equilateral: three equal sides.
  • Isosceles: two equal sides.
  • Scalene: all sides are different.

Types by Angles

  • Acute triangle: all angles are less than 90°.
  • Right triangle: one angle is 90°.
  • Obtuse triangle: one angle is greater than 90°.

Area of a Triangle

Area = ½ × base × height
Example:
If the base is 10 cm and the height is 6 cm:

Area = ½ × 10 × 6 = 30 cm²

5. Quadrilaterals

A quadrilateral is a polygon with four sides.

The interior angles of a quadrilateral add up to 360°.

A + B + C + D = 360°

Square

A square has four equal sides and four right angles.

Rectangle

A rectangle has four right angles. Opposite sides are equal and parallel.

Parallelogram

A parallelogram has two pairs of parallel opposite sides.

Rhombus

A rhombus has four equal sides.

Trapezium

A trapezium has at least one pair of parallel sides.

Kite

A kite has two pairs of adjacent equal sides.

6. Polygons

A polygon is a closed two-dimensional shape made from straight line segments.

Common Polygons

  • 3 sides — Triangle
  • 4 sides — Quadrilateral
  • 5 sides — Pentagon
  • 6 sides — Hexagon
  • 7 sides — Heptagon
  • 8 sides — Octagon
  • 9 sides — Nonagon
  • 10 sides — Decagon

Interior Angle Sum

The sum of the interior angles of an n-sided polygon is:

(n - 2) × 180°

Regular Polygons

A regular polygon has equal sides and equal interior angles.

The size of each interior angle of a regular polygon is:

[(n - 2) × 180°] ÷ n

7. Circles

A circle is a two-dimensional shape in which every point on the circumference is the same distance from the centre.

Radius

The radius is the distance from the centre of a circle to its circumference.

Diameter

The diameter passes through the centre and connects two points on the circumference.

Diameter = 2 × Radius

Circumference

C = 2πr

Area

A = πr²

Chord

A chord is a line segment connecting two points on a circle.

Arc

An arc is part of the circumference of a circle.

8. Perimeter and Area

Perimeter is the total distance around a two-dimensional shape.

Area is the amount of space covered inside a two-dimensional shape.

Square

Perimeter = 4s
Area = s²

Rectangle

Perimeter = 2(l + w)
Area = l × w

Parallelogram

Area = base × height

Triangle

Area = ½ × base × height

Trapezium

Area = ½(a + b)h

Circle

Area = πr²
Circumference = 2πr

9. Volume and Surface Area

Volume measures the amount of three-dimensional space occupied by an object.

Cube

Volume = s³

Cuboid

Volume = l × w × h

Cylinder

Volume = πr²h

Prism

Volume = Cross-sectional Area × Length

Surface Area

Surface area is the total area of all the external surfaces of a three-dimensional object.

10. Pythagoras' Theorem

Pythagoras' theorem applies to right-angled triangles. The longest side is called the hypotenuse.

a² + b² = c²

Here, c represents the hypotenuse.

Example:
If the two shorter sides are 3 cm and 4 cm:

c² = 3² + 4²
c² = 9 + 16
c² = 25
c = 5 cm

11. Similarity and Congruence

Congruent Shapes

Two shapes are congruent when they have exactly the same shape and size.

Similar Shapes

Similar shapes have the same shape but may have different sizes. Corresponding sides have the same scale factor.

Scale Factor

The scale factor tells us how much a shape has been enlarged or reduced.

Scale Factor = New Length ÷ Original Length

12. Transformations

A transformation changes the position, orientation or size of a shape.

Translation

A translation moves a shape from one position to another without rotating or changing its size.

Reflection

A reflection produces a mirror image of a shape across a line of reflection.

Rotation

A rotation turns a shape around a fixed point called the centre of rotation.

Enlargement

An enlargement changes the size of a shape according to a scale factor.

13. Coordinate Geometry

Coordinate geometry uses numbers to describe the position of points and shapes on a coordinate plane.

Coordinates

A point is written as:

(x, y)

The first value represents the horizontal position and the second value represents the vertical position.

Distance Between Two Points

d = √[(x₂ - x₁)² + (y₂ - y₁)²]

Midpoint

M = ((x₁ + x₂)/2, (y₁ + y₂)/2)

Gradient

m = (y₂ - y₁) / (x₂ - x₁)
