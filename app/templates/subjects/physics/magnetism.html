Introduction to Magnetic Fields

Magnetism is a fundamental force phenomenon associated with moving electric charges and intrinsic magnetic dipole moments of elementary particles. Unlike electric charges, which can exist as isolated monopoles (single positive or negative charges), magnetic poles always appear in inseparable north-south pairs (dipoles). A magnetic field (B) is a vector field that exerts forces on moving charges and permanent magnets.

Core Conceptual Principle:
Magnetic fields do not exert forces on stationary electric charges. A magnetic force acts only on electric charges that are in motion, and its direction is always perpendicular to both the velocity vector of the charge and the magnetic field vector.
02

The Lorentz Force on Moving Charges

When an electric charge (q) moves with velocity vector (v) through a region containing both an electric field (E) and a magnetic field (B), it experiences a combined electromagnetic force known as the Lorentz Force:

  • Lorentz Force Equation:
    Force = Electric Force + Magnetic Force (F = q * E + q * (v × B)).
  • Magnitude of Magnetic Force:
    Magnetic Force = Charge × Speed × Magnetic Field Strength × sine of Angle (FB = |q| * v * B * sin(theta)), where theta is the angle between the velocity vector and the magnetic field vector. Measured in Newtons (N).
  • Circular Motion in Uniform Magnetic Fields: Because the magnetic force is always perpendicular to velocity, it performs zero mechanical work. It causes moving charges to travel in uniform circular orbits with a radius given by: Radius = (Mass × Speed) / (Charge Magnitude × Magnetic Field) (r = (m * v) / (|q| * B)).
03

Magnetic Force on Current-Carrying Conductors

Because electric current consists of a stream of moving charge carriers within a wire, a current-carrying wire placed inside a magnetic field also experiences a net magnetic force.

  • Magnetic Force Equation on a Straight Wire:
    Force = Current × Length Vector crossed into Magnetic Field Vector (F = I * (L × B)).
  • Magnitude Equation:
    Force = Current × Length of Wire in Field × Magnetic Field Strength × sine of Angle (F = I * L * B * sin(theta)).
  • Right-Hand Rule: The direction of the magnetic force is determined by pointing the fingers of the right hand in the direction of conventional current (I), curling them toward the magnetic field lines (B), and extending the thumb to point in the direction of the resulting magnetic force (F).
04

Sources of Magnetic Fields & Ampère's Law

Electric currents produce magnetic fields in the space surrounding them. Ampère's Law relates the magnetic field circulating around any closed loop to the total electric current passing through the surface bounded by that loop.

  • Ampère's Law Equation: The line integral of the magnetic field vector around a closed path equals the permeability of free space multiplied by the net enclosed current:
    Line Integral of Magnetic Field • differential length = Permittivity Constant × Enclosed Current (Integral of (B • dl) = mu0 * Ienclosed), where permeability of free space mu0 = 4 × pi × 10-7 T·m/A.
  • Magnetic Field of a Long Straight Wire: Applying Ampère's Law to a circular path of radius r around a long straight wire carrying current I yields:
    Magnetic Field = (Permittivity Constant × Current) / (2 × pi × Distance) (B = (mu0 * I) / (2 * pi * r)).
  • Magnetic Field inside a Solenoid: For a tightly wound solenoid having n turns per unit length carrying current I:
    Magnetic Field = Permittivity Constant × Turns per Unit Length × Current (B = mu0 * n * I).

Exhaustive Worked Exam Examples

Problem 1 (Magnetic Force on a Moving Charge):

An electron is injected into a uniform magnetic field of strength B = 0.500 Tesla with a speed of v = 2.00 × 106 m/s. The velocity vector of the electron is perpendicular to the magnetic field direction (theta = 90 degrees). Calculate the magnitude of the magnetic force acting on the electron. (Given that the elementary charge of an electron is e = 1.602 × 10-19 Coulombs).

Detailed Step-by-Step Solution:
- Formula: FB = |q| * v * B * sin(theta).
- Since theta = 90 degrees, sin(90 degrees) = 1.
- Substitute values:
FB = (1.602 × 10-19 C) × (2.00 × 106 m/s) × (0.500 T) × 1.
- Numerator calculation: 1.602 × 2.00 × 0.500 = 1.602.
- Exponent calculation: 10-19 × 106 = 10-13.
- Result: FB = 1.60 × 10-13 Newtons.
Problem 2 (Magnetic Force on a Current-Carrying Wire):

A straight horizontal copper wire of length L = 0.400 m carries a direct current of I = 15.0 Amperes. The wire is placed perpendicular (theta = 90 degrees) inside a uniform magnetic field of strength B = 0.250 Tesla. Calculate the magnitude of the magnetic force exerted on the wire.

Detailed Step-by-Step Solution:
- Formula: F = I * L * B * sin(theta).
- Since theta = 90 degrees, sin(90 degrees) = 1.
- Substitute values:
F = (15.0 A) × (0.400 m) × (0.250 T) × 1.
- Multiplication: 15.0 × 0.400 = 6.00.
- 6.00 × 0.250 = 1.50 Newtons.
Problem 3 (Magnetic Field of a Long Straight Wire):

Calculate the magnitude of the magnetic field produced at a radial distance of r = 0.0500 meters away from a long, straight, thin wire carrying a direct electric current of I = 10.0 Amperes. (Use mu0 = 4 × pi × 10-7 T·m/A).

Detailed Step-by-Step Solution:
- Formula: B = (mu0 * I) / (2 * pi * r).
- Substitute values:
B = ((4 * pi × 10-7 T·m/A) × (10.0 A)) / (2 * pi × 0.0500 m).
- Simplify the pi terms: (4 × pi) / (2 × pi) = 2.
- B = (2 × 10-7 × 10.0) / 0.0500 = (2.00 × 10-6) / 0.0500.
- Division: B = 4.00 × 10-5 Tesla = 40.0 microteslas.
