Introduction to Geometrical Optics

Geometrical optics (or ray optics) is the approximation in physics where light is modeled as imaginary straight-line rays traveling through space. This approximation is valid when the physical objects and apertures encountered by the light are much larger than the wavelength of the light, allowing wave diffraction effects to be ignored.

Core Conceptual Principle:
Light rays travel in straight lines in a homogeneous, isotropic medium and obey the laws of reflection and refraction when interacting with optical boundaries.
02

Laws of Reflection

When a ray of light strikes a smooth reflective surface (such as a flat mirror), it bounces off according to two strict geometric rules:

  • First Law: The incident ray, the reflected ray, and the normal line perpendicular to the surface at the point of incidence all lie in the exact same plane.
  • Second Law: The angle of reflection (thetar) measured relative to the surface normal is always equal to the angle of incidence (thetai):
    Angle of Reflection = Angle of Incidence (thetar = thetai).
  • Plane Mirror Images: Images formed by flat mirrors are virtual, upright, located exactly as far behind the mirror as the object is in front of it, and exhibit lateral inversion (left-right reversal).
03

Refraction and Snell's Law

When light travels from one transparent medium into another with a different optical density, its speed changes, causing the light ray to bend at the boundary. This phenomenon is known as refraction.

  • Index of Refraction (n): Defined as the ratio of the speed of light in a vacuum (c) to the speed of light in the medium (v):
    Refractive Index = Speed of Light in Vacuum / Speed of Light in Medium (n = c / v). (For vacuum, n = 1.00; for water, n approximately equal to 1.33; for glass, n approximately equal to 1.50).
  • Snell's Law of Refraction: Relates the angles of incidence and refraction to the refractive indices of the two media:
    Refractive Index 1 × sine(Angle of Incidence) = Refractive Index 2 × sine(Angle of Refraction)
    (n1 × sin(theta1) = n2 × sin(theta2)).
04

Total Internal Reflection (TIR)

When light travels from a denser medium (higher refractive index n1) to a less dense medium (lower refractive index n2), the refracted ray bends away from the normal. As the angle of incidence increases, the angle of refraction reaches 90 degrees.

  • Critical Angle (thetac): The specific angle of incidence that produces an angle of refraction of exactly 90 degrees:
    Critical Angle = inverse sine of (Refractive Index of Outer Medium / Refractive Index of Inner Medium)
    (thetac = arcsin(n2 / n1)).
  • Total Internal Reflection: If the angle of incidence exceeds the critical angle, no light refracts out into the second medium; instead, 100 percent of the light reflects internally. This principle powers fiber optic communication cables and diamond brilliance.
05

Spherical Mirrors

Spherical mirrors are polished reflective surfaces shaped like a section of a sphere. They are categorized into concave (converging) and convex (diverging) mirrors.

  • Focal Length (f): For spherical mirrors of radius of curvature R, the focal length is equal to half the radius:
    Focal Length = Radius of Curvature / 2 (f = R / 2).
  • Mirror Equation: Relates object distance (do), image distance (di), and focal length (f):
    (1 / Object Distance) + (1 / Image Distance) = (1 / Focal Length) ((1 / do) + (1 / di) = (1 / f)).
  • Magnification (m): Defines the relative size and orientation of the formed image:
    Magnification = Image Height / Object Height = - (Image Distance / Object Distance) (m = hi / ho = - (di / do)).
06

Thin Lenses and Refraction

A thin lens is a transparent optical medium bounded by two spherical surfaces (or one flat and one spherical surface). Convex lenses converge incoming parallel light rays to a focal point, whereas concave lenses diverge them.

  • Thin Lens Equation: Identical in mathematical form to the mirror equation:
    (1 / Object Distance) + (1 / Image Distance) = (1 / Focal Length).
  • Lensmaker's Equation: Relates the focal length of a lens to its refractive index (n) and the radii of curvature of its two surfaces (R1 and R2):
    (1 / Focal Length) = (Refractive Index - 1) × ((1 / Radius 1) - (1 / Radius 2))
  • Lens Power (P): Measured in diopters (D) where focal length must be in meters:
    Power = 1 / Focal Length (P = 1 / f).

Exhaustive Worked Exam Examples

Problem 1 (Snell's Law Calculation):

A light ray traveling in air (n1 = 1.00) strikes a flat glass block (n2 = 1.50) at an angle of incidence of 45.0 degrees. Calculate the angle of refraction (theta2) inside the glass.

Detailed Step-by-Step Solution:
- Snell's Law: n1 * sin(theta1) = n2 * sin(theta2).
- Rearrange for sin(theta2): sin(theta2) = (n1 / n2) * sin(theta1).
- sin(theta2) = (1.00 / 1.50) × sin(45.0 degrees) = 0.6667 × 0.7071 = 0.4714.
- Take inverse sine: theta2 = arcsin(0.4714) approximately equal to 28.1 degrees.
Problem 2 (Thin Lens Image Formation):

An object of height 4.0 cm is placed 30.0 cm in front of a converging thin convex lens having a focal length of f = 10.0 cm. Calculate: (a) the image distance (di), and (b) the height of the formed image.

Detailed Step-by-Step Solution:
(a) Image Distance (di):
- Thin Lens Equation: (1 / do) + (1 / di) = (1 / f) implies (1 / di) = (1 / f) - (1 / do).
- (1 / di) = (1 / 10.0) - (1 / 30.0) = (3 / 30.0) - (1 / 30.0) = 2 / 30.0 = 1 / 15.0.
- di = +15.0 cm (real, inverted image formed on the opposite side of the lens).

(b) Image Height (hi):
- Magnification m = - (di / do) = - (15.0 cm / 30.0 cm) = -0.50.
- hi = m × ho = (-0.50) × (4.0 cm) = -2.0 cm (inverted image of height 2.0 cm).
Problem 3 (Critical Angle Calculation):

Calculate the critical angle for total internal reflection when light travels from crown glass (refractive index n1 = 1.52) into surrounding air (refractive index n2 = 1.00).

Detailed Step-by-Step Solution:
- Critical Angle formula: thetac = arcsin(n2 / n1).
- Substitute values: thetac = arcsin(1.00 / 1.52) = arcsin(0.6579).
- thetac approximately equal to 41.1 degrees.
