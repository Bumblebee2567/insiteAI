01

Introduction to Rotation

Up to this point, our study of mechanics has largely treated objects as point particles undergoing translational motion. However, real-world objects have finite spatial dimensions and can rotate about a fixed axis or a moving center of mass. Rotational dynamics is the study of how torques create angular acceleration, mirroring the role of linear forces in Newton's laws.

The Translational-Rotational Analogy:
Almost every linear quantity has a direct rotational counterpart: position (x) becomes angular position (theta), velocity (v) becomes angular velocity (omega), mass (m) becomes moment of inertia (I), and force (F) becomes torque (tau).
02

Angular Kinematics and Variables

When a rigid body rotates about a fixed axis, every particle in the body moves in a circular path centered on that axis.

  • Angular Position (theta): Measured in radians (rad), representing the angle swept out by a reference line relative to a fixed zero axis.
  • Angular Velocity (omega): The rate of change of angular position: omega = d(theta) / dt. Measured in rad/s.
  • Angular Acceleration (alpha): The rate of change of angular velocity: alpha = d(omega) / dt = d2(theta) / dt2. Measured in rad/s2.
  • Tangential Relations: For a point at a radial distance r from the rotation axis:
    • Tangential speed: v = r × omega
    • Tangential acceleration: at = r × alpha
    • Centripetal (radial) acceleration: ac = omega2 × r = v2 / r
  • Kinematic Equations (Constant Alpha):
    • omega = omega0 + alpha * t
    • delta theta = omega0 * t + (1/2) * alpha * t2
    • omega2 = omega02 + 2 * alpha * delta theta
03

Torque and Rotational Equilibrium

Torque (tau) is the rotational analog of force; it is a measure of the effectiveness of a force in producing rotation about an axis.

  • Definition of Torque: vector tau = vector r cross vector F → Magnitude: tau = r * F * sin(phi) = F * rperp
    where r is the position vector from the pivot to the point of application, F is the applied force, phi is the angle between them, and rperp is the perpendicular lever arm (moment arm).
  • Units: Newton-meters (N·m).
  • Rotational Equilibrium: For a rigid body to be in complete mechanical equilibrium, two conditions must be satisfied simultaneously:
    1. Translational Equilibrium: sum of vector F = 0
    2. Rotational Equilibrium: sum of vector tau = 0 about any chosen pivot point.
04

Moment of Inertia and Rotational Inertia

The moment of inertia (I) is the rotational analog of mass. It measures a body's resistance to changes in its rotational motion. Unlike mass, moment of inertia depends not only on the total mass of the object but also on how that mass is distributed relative to the chosen axis of rotation.

  • Discrete Particles: I = sum of (mi * ri2).
  • Continuous Bodies: I = integral of (r2 dm), evaluated over the volume or area. Measured in kg·m2.
  • Parallel Axis Theorem: If the moment of inertia about the center of mass is Icm, the moment of inertia about any parallel axis separated by a distance d is:
    I = Icm + M * d2

Common Moments of Inertia

Object / Shape Axis of Rotation Moment of Inertia (I)

05

Rotational Kinetic Energy

A rotating rigid body possesses kinetic energy because its constituent mass elements are in motion.

  • Rotational Kinetic Energy: Krot = (1/2) * sum of (mi vi2) = (1/2) * I * omega2.
  • Total Kinetic Energy of Rolling Objects: An object that is both translating and rotating (like a rolling wheel) possesses both translational and rotational kinetic energy:
    Ktotal = Ktrans + Krot = (1/2) * M * vcm2 + (1/2) * I * omega2
06

Rolling Motion Without Slipping

For a round object (such as a cylinder, sphere, or hoop) rolling across a surface without slipping, there is a strict kinematic constraint linking its linear translational speed vcm and its angular speed omega:

Condition for Rolling Without Slipping:
vcm = omega * R  and  acm = alpha * R
where R is the radius of the rolling object. The point of contact with the ground is momentarily at rest (instantaneous center of rotation).
07

Angular Momentum and Conservation

Angular momentum (vector L) is the rotational analog of linear momentum.

  • Definition for a Rigid Body: vector L = I * vector omega (for a symmetric rigid body rotating about a fixed principal axis).
  • Definition for a Particle: vector L = vector r cross vector p = m * (vector r cross vector v).
  • Newton's Second Law for Rotation: sum of vector tau = d(vector L) / dt.
  • Conservation of Angular Momentum: If the net external torque acting on a system is zero (sum of vector tauext = 0), the total angular momentum of the system remains strictly constant:
    Ii * omegai = If * omegaf (e.g., an ice skater pulling in their arms to spin faster).

Exhaustive Worked Exam Examples

Problem 1 (Rotational Kinematics & Torque):

A solid disk of mass M = 4.00 kg and radius R = 0.20 m is free to rotate about a frictionless fixed central axle. A light cord is wrapped around the rim of the disk and pulled with a constant tension force F = 15.0 N. (a) Calculate the torque exerted on the disk. (b) Calculate the angular acceleration of the disk. (c) Calculate the angular speed of the disk after 3.00 seconds starting from rest.

Detailed Step-by-Step Solution:
(a) Torque ($\tau$):
- $\tau = R \cdot F \sin(90^\circ) = (0.20\text{ m})(15.0\text{ N}) =$ $3.00\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$.

(b) Angular Acceleration ($\alpha$):
- For a solid disk, $I = \frac{1}{2}M R^2 = \frac{1}{2}(4.00\text{ kg})(0.20\text{ m})^2 = 0.5(4.00)(0.04) = 0.08\text{ kg}\cdot\text{m}^2$.
- Using $\tau = I \alpha \implies \alpha = \frac{\tau}{I} = \frac{3.00}{0.08} =$ $37.5\text{ rad/s}^2$.

(c) Angular Speed ($\omega$ after $t = 3.00\text{ s}$):
- $\omega = \omega_0 + \alpha t = 0 + (37.5\text{ rad/s}^2)(3.00\text{ s}) =$ $112.5\text{ rad/s}$.
Problem 2 (Rolling Down an Inclined Plane):

A solid uniform sphere of mass M = 2.00 kg and radius R = 0.10 m is released from rest at the top of an inclined plane of height h = 1.50 m. It rolls down the incline without slipping. Calculate the linear speed of the sphere's center of mass when it reaches the bottom of the incline. (Assume $g = 9.81\text{ m/s}^2$).

Detailed Step-by-Step Solution:
- Conservation of Mechanical Energy: $E_i = E_f \implies M g h = K_{trans} + K_{rot}$.
- $M g h = \frac{1}{2}M v^2 + \frac{1}{2}I \omega^2$.
- For a solid sphere, $I = \frac{2}{5}M R^2$, and for rolling without slipping, $\omega = \frac{v}{R}$.
- Substitute $I$ and $\omega$:
$M g h = \frac{1}{2}M v^2 + \frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{2}{5}M R^2\right)\left(\frac{v}{R}\right)^2 = \frac{1}{2}M v^2 + \frac{1}{5}M v^2$.
- Combine fractions: $M g h = \left(\frac{1}{2} + \frac{1}{5}\right)M v^2 = \frac{7}{10}M v^2$.
- Mass $M$ cancels out: $g h = \frac{7}{10}v^2 \implies v = \sqrt{\frac{10}{7}g h}$.
- $v = \sqrt{\frac{10}{7}(9.81)(1.50)} = \sqrt{\frac{147.15}{7}} = \sqrt{21.02} \approx$ 4.58 m/s.
Problem 3 (Conservation of Angular Momentum):

A spinning disk of moment of inertia $I_1 = 2.00\text{ kg}\cdot\text{m}^2$ rotates freely at an angular speed $\omega_1 = 5.00\text{ rad/s}$ on a frictionless vertical axle. A second identical non-rotating disk of moment of inertia $I_2 = 2.00\text{ kg}\cdot\text{m}^2$ is dropped directly on top of the first disk, and they couple together to rotate as a single unit. Calculate the final common angular speed of the combined disks.

Detailed Step-by-Step Solution:
- Conservation of Angular Momentum ($L_i = L_f$):
- $I_1 \omega_1 + I_2 \omega_2 = (I_1 + I_2)\omega_f$.
- $(2.00\text{ kg}\cdot\text{m}^2)(5.00\text{ rad/s}) + (2.00)(0) = (2.00 + 2.00)\omega_f$.
- $10.0 = 4.00 \omega_f \implies \omega_f = \frac{10.0}{4.00} =$ 2.50 rad/s.
