Introduction to Physical Optics

While geometrical optics treats light as straight-line rays, physical optics (or wave optics) studies light explicitly as electromagnetic waves. Phenomena such as interference, diffraction, and polarization cannot be explained by ray optics alone; they require an understanding of how light waves overlap, superimpose, and interact with microscopic apertures and boundaries.

Core Conceptual Principle:
Light exhibits wave-particle duality. In physical optics, the wave nature of light dominates when light encounters obstacles or openings comparable in size to its wavelength.
02

Huygens' Principle and Wave Propagation

Huygens' Principle is a geometrical construction method created by Christiaan Huygens to determine how a wave front propagates through space:

  • Fundamental Statement: Every point on a wavefront acts as a source of secondary spherical wavelets. After a time interval, the new position of the wavefront is the envelope (tangent surface) tangent to all these secondary wavelets.
  • Application: Huygens' principle provides a rigorous physical explanation for the laws of reflection and refraction, as well as why light bends (diffracts) around sharp edges and corners.
03

Young's Double-Slit Interference

Thomas Young's historic double-slit experiment provided undeniable proof of the wave nature of light by demonstrating that coherent light passing through two narrow slits produces an interference pattern of alternating bright and dark fringes on a viewing screen.

  • Path Length Difference (Delta L): The difference in distance traveled by light waves from two slits separated by distance d meeting at an angle theta is:
    Path Difference = Slit Separation × sine of Angle (Delta L = d × sin(theta)).
  • Constructive Interference (Bright Fringes): Occurs when the path difference is an integer multiple of the wavelength:
    d × sine(theta) = Order Number × Wavelength (d × sin(theta) = m × lambda, where m = 0, 1, 2, ...).
  • Destructive Interference (Dark Fringes): Occurs when the path difference is a half-integer multiple of the wavelength:
    d × sine(theta) = (Order Number + 0.5) × Wavelength (d × sin(theta) = (m + 0.5) × lambda).
04

Thin-Film Interference

The shimmering rainbow colors seen on soap bubbles and oil slicks on water are caused by thin-film interference. Light reflecting off the top and bottom boundaries of a transparent thin film interfere constructively or destructively depending on film thickness and wavelength.

  • Phase Change on Reflection: When a light wave reflects from a boundary of a medium having a higher refractive index than the medium it is leaving, it undergoes a 180-degree phase shift (equivalent to a half-wavelength shift, lambda / 2). If reflecting from a lower refractive index medium, no phase shift occurs.
  • Constructive Interference Condition for Thin Films (with one phase shift):
    2 × Refractive Index × Film Thickness = (Order Number + 0.5) × Wavelength (2 * n * t = (m + 0.5) × lambda).
05

Diffraction and Diffraction Gratings

Diffraction is the bending of light waves around the edges of apertures or obstacles. A diffraction grating is an optical component containing thousands of closely spaced parallel slits per centimeter used to separate light into its component spectral colors with high precision.

  • Grating Equation: Relates the slit spacing (d), diffraction angle (theta), order number (m), and wavelength (lambda):
    Slit Spacing × sine(Angle) = Order Number × Wavelength (d × sin(theta) = m × lambda).
06

Electromagnetic Polarization

Light is a transverse electromagnetic wave consisting of mutually perpendicular oscillating electric and magnetic fields. Polarization refers to the orientation of the electric field vector oscillations.

  • Unpolarized Light: Light waves whose electric field vectors vibrate in all possible directions perpendicular to the direction of propagation (such as sunlight or incandescent bulbs).
  • Polarizing Filters (Malus's Law): When unpolarized light passes through an ideal polarizing filter, its intensity is reduced by half. If polarized light of intensity I0 passes through a second polarizing filter (analyzer) whose transmission axis is at an angle theta to the first, the transmitted intensity obeys Malus's Law:
    Transmitted Intensity = Initial Intensity × cosine squared of Angle (I = I0 × cos2(theta)).
  • Brewster's Law: When unpolarized light strikes a reflective dielectric surface at a specific angle known as Brewster's angle (thetaB), the reflected light is 100 percent polarized parallel to the surface:
    tangent of Brewster Angle = Refractive Index of Second Medium / Refractive Index of First Medium (tan(thetaB) = n2 / n1).

Exhaustive Worked Exam Examples

Problem 1 (Young's Double-Slit Experiment):

Monochromatic light of wavelength 550 nm is shone onto two narrow slits separated by 0.20 mm. The viewing screen is located at a distance of 1.50 m from the slits. Calculate: (a) the angular position of the second-order bright fringe (m = 2), and (b) the linear distance of this fringe from the central maximum on the screen.

Detailed Step-by-Step Solution:
(a) Angular Position (theta):
- Formula: d × sin(theta) = m × lambda.
- Convert wavelength to meters: lambda = 550 nm = 5.50 × 10-7 m.
- Slit separation d = 0.20 mm = 2.00 × 10-4 m.
- sin(theta) = (m × lambda) / d = (2 × 5.50 × 10-7 m) / (2.00 × 10-4 m) = 1.10 × 10-6 / 2.00 × 10-4 = 0.0055.
- theta = arcsin(0.0055) approximately equal to 0.315 degrees.

(b) Linear Distance on Screen (y):
- Using small angle approximation (y = L × tan(theta) ≈ L × sin(theta) for small angles):
- y = (1.50 m) × 0.0055 = 0.00825 meters = 8.25 mm.
Problem 2 (Diffraction Grating Resolution):

A diffraction grating has 5000 lines per centimeter. Monochromatic light is shone onto the grating, producing a first-order bright fringe (m = 1) at an angle of 17.5 degrees. Calculate the wavelength of the incident light in nanometers.

Detailed Step-by-Step Solution:
- Calculate slit spacing (d): 5000 lines per centimeter means 500,000 lines per meter.
- d = 1 / 500,000 = 2.00 × 10-6 meters (2.00 micrometers).
- Grating equation: d × sin(theta) = m × lambda implies lambda = (d × sin(theta)) / m.
- lambda = (2.00 × 10-6 m) × sin(17.5 degrees) / 1 = (2.00 × 10-6) × 0.3007 = 6.014 × 10-7 meters.
- Convert to nanometers: lambda = 6.014 × 10-7 m × 109 nm/m = 601.4 nm.
Problem 3 (Brewster's Angle Calculation):

Calculate the polarizing Brewster's angle for light reflecting off a flat pool of water given that the refractive index of water is n = 1.33 and the refractive index of air is n = 1.00.

Detailed Step-by-Step Solution:
- Brewster's Law formula: tan(thetaB) = n2 / n1.
- tan(thetaB) = 1.33 / 1.00 = 1.33.
- Take inverse tangent: thetaB = arctan(1.33) approximately equal to 53.1 degrees.
