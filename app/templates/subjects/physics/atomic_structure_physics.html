01

Introduction to Atomic Structure

Atomic physics investigates the internal structure of atoms, the interactions between atomic nuclei and orbiting electrons, and the emission and absorption of electromagnetic radiation by atomic systems. Understanding atomic spectra provided the crucial empirical foundation that led to the development of modern quantum mechanics.

Core Conceptual Principle:
Atoms are not indivisible elementary particles; they consist of a dense, positively charged nucleus surrounded by negatively charged electrons occupying quantized energy states.
02

Rutherford's Nuclear Model

In 1911, Ernest Rutherford, along with Hans Geiger and Ernest Marsden, performed the historic gold foil scattering experiment. By firing alpha particles (helium nuclei) at extremely thin gold foil, they observed that while most alpha particles passed straight through, a tiny fraction were deflected at very large angles, and some bounced almost straight back.

  • The Nuclear Atom Conclusion: Rutherford concluded that an atom's positive charge and nearly all its mass are concentrated in a microscopic central region called the nucleus, while electrons orbit at a vast distance in empty space.
  • Classical Physics Failure: Classical electrodynamics predicted that orbiting electrons (accelerating charges) would continuously radiate electromagnetic waves, spiral inward, and collapse into the nucleus within a fraction of a nanosecond, proving that classical mechanics could not explain stable atoms.
03

The Bohr Model of Hydrogen

In 1913, Niels Bohr successfully resolved the atomic collapse problem by combining classical mechanics with Planck's quantum hypothesis, proposing a semi-quantum model specifically for single-electron hydrogen atoms.

  • Bohr's Postulate of Quantized Angular Momentum: Electrons can only orbit the nucleus in specific non-radiating circular orbits where their orbital angular momentum is an integer multiple of reduced Planck's constant (hbar = h / (2 * pi)):
    Angular Momentum = Principal Quantum Number × Reduced Planck's Constant (L = me * v * r = n * hbar, where n = 1, 2, 3, ...).
  • Quantized Energy Levels of Hydrogen:
    Energy Level n = - (13.6 Electron-Volts) / (Principal Quantum Number squared) (En = -13.6 eV / n2).
  • Photon Emission/Absorption: When an electron drops from a higher energy level (nhigh) to a lower level (nlow), it emits a single photon whose energy equals the energy difference between the states: Photon Energy = Energy High - Energy Low = Planck's Constant × Frequency (E = Ehigh - Elow = h * f).
04

Hydrogen Emission Spectra & Spectral Series

Excited hydrogen atoms emit light at specific discrete wavelengths known as spectral lines. These lines are organized into distinct mathematical series depending on the lower energy level (nf) to which electrons transition.

Spectral Series Lower Energy Level (nf) Upper Energy Level Transitions (ni) Electromagnetic Spectrum Region
Lyman Series nf = 1 ni = 2, 3, 4, ... Ultraviolet (UV)
Balmer Series nf = 2 ni = 3, 4, 5, ... Visible Light & Near-UV
Paschen Series nf = 3 ni = 4, 5, 6, ... Infrared (IR)
Brackett Series nf = 4 ni = 5, 6, 7, ... Infrared (IR)
Rydberg Equation for Hydrogen Wavelengths:
1 / Wavelength = Rydberg Constant × ((1 / Final Level squared) - (1 / Initial Level squared))
(1 / lambda = RH × ((1 / nf2) - (1 / ni2))),
where Rydberg constant RH ≈ 1.097 × 107 m-1.
05

X-Ray Production & Characteristic X-Rays

X-rays are high-frequency, highly penetrating electromagnetic waves produced when high-speed, accelerated electrons strike a heavy metal target (such as tungsten) inside an evacuated X-ray tube.

  • Bremsstrahlung (Braking Radiation): When high-speed electrons are rapidly decelerated by the electric fields of atomic nuclei in the target metal, their lost kinetic energy is converted into continuous-spectrum X-ray photons. The shortest wavelength produced (cutoff wavelength lambdamin) corresponds to an electron losing 100 percent of its kinetic energy in a single collision:
    Minimum Wavelength = (Planck's Constant × Speed of Light) / Accelerating Voltage (lambdamin = (h * c) / (e * V)).
  • Characteristic X-Rays: Produced when an incident high-speed electron knocks out an inner-shell electron (such as the K-shell) of a target atom, causing an outer-shell electron to drop down and fill the vacancy while emitting an X-ray photon of precise, discrete characteristic frequency.

Exhaustive Worked Exam Examples

Problem 1 (Bohr Hydrogen Energy Level Transition):

Calculate the energy (in electron-volts) and wavelength (in nanometers) of the photon emitted when an electron in a hydrogen atom makes a transition from the third excited state (ni = 4) to the ground state (nf = 1). (Use En = -13.6 eV / n2, h * c = 1240 eV·nm).

Detailed Step-by-Step Solution:
(a) Calculate Initial and Final Energies:
- Ground state energy (n = 1): E1 = -13.6 / (1)2 = -13.6 eV.
- Third excited state (n = 4): E4 = -13.6 / (4)2 = -13.6 / 16 = -0.85 eV.

(b) Photon Energy (Ephoton):
- Ephoton = E4 - E1 = -0.85 eV - (-13.6 eV) = 12.75 eV.

(c) Photon Wavelength (lambda):
- Using hc = 1240 eV·nm: lambda = (1240 eV·nm) / (12.75 eV) ≈ 97.3 nanometers (Lyman series UV photon).
Problem 2 (Balmer Series Wavelength Calculation):

Calculate the wavelength of the first line of the Balmer series in hydrogen (transition from ni = 3 to nf = 2). (Use Rydberg constant RH = 1.097 × 107 m-1).

Detailed Step-by-Step Solution:
- Rydberg formula: (1 / lambda) = RH * ((1 / nf2) - (1 / ni2)).
- Substitute nf = 2 and ni = 3:
(1 / lambda) = (1.097 × 107 m-1) × ((1 / 22) - (1 / 32)) = (1.097 × 107) × ((1 / 4) - (1 / 9)).
- (1 / 4) - (1 / 9) = 0.250 - 0.1111 = 0.1389.
- (1 / lambda) = (1.097 × 107) × 0.1389 = 1.5238 × 106 m-1.
- Invert to find wavelength: lambda = 1 / (1.5238 × 106 m-1) = 6.562 × 10-7 meters = 656 nanometers (H-alpha red visible line).
Problem 3 (Minimum X-Ray Wavelength):

An X-ray tube operates at an accelerating voltage of V = 50.0 kV (50,000 Volts). Calculate the minimum cutoff wavelength (lambdamin) of the X-rays produced. (Given h = 6.626 × 10-34 J·s, c = 3.00 × 108 m/s, e = 1.602 × 10-19 C).

Detailed Step-by-Step Solution:
- Formula: lambdamin = (h * c) / (e * V).
- Numerator (h * c): (6.626 × 10-34) × (3.00 × 108) = 1.9878 × 10-25 J·m.
- Denominator (e * V): (1.602 × 10-19 C) × (50,000 V) = 8.01 × 10-15 Joules.
- Division: lambdamin = (1.9878 × 10-25) / (8.01 × 10-15) = 2.482 × 10-11 meters = 0.0248 nanometers (0.248 Angstroms).
