01

Introduction to Electric Charge and Electrostatics

Electrostatics is the branch of physics that studies electric charges at rest. Electric charge is an intrinsic fundamental property of subatomic particles, categorized into positive (protons) and negative (electrons). The fundamental unit of charge magnitude is the elementary charge (e ≈ 1.602 × 10-19 Coulombs).

Core Conservation Principle:
Electric charge is strictly conserved. The total net electric charge of an isolated physical system can never be created or destroyed. Like charges repel one another, whereas opposite charges attract.
02

Coulomb's Law of Electrostatic Force

Formulated by Charles-Augustin de Coulomb, Coulomb's Law quantifies the electrostatic force between two stationary point charges:

Coulomb's Law Equation:
Electrostatic Force = Coulomb Constant × ((Magnitude of Charge 1 × Magnitude of Charge 2) / Distance squared)
F = ke × (|q1| × |q2|) / r2,
where Coulomb constant ke = 1 / (4 × pi × permittivity of free space) ≈ 8.99 × 109 N·m2/C2.
  • Vector Nature: Electrostatic force is a vector quantity directed along the straight line joining the two point charges. Forces are repulsive for charges of the same sign and attractive for opposite signs.
03

Electric Fields and Field Lines

An electric field (E) is a vector field surrounding any charged particle that exerts an electrostatic force on other charges in its vicinity. The electric field vector at any point in space is defined as the electrostatic force experienced by a tiny positive test charge placed at that point, divided by the test charge magnitude:

  • Definition Equation:
    Electric Field = Electrostatric Force / Test Charge (E = F / q0), measured in Newtons per Coulomb (N/C) or Volts per meter (V/m).
  • Electric Field of a Point Charge:
    Electric Field = Coulomb Constant × (Magnitude of Source Charge / Distance squared) (E = ke × |q| / r2).
  • Field Line Rules: Electric field lines originate on positive charges and terminate on negative charges. The density of field lines is proportional to the local magnitude of the electric field strength.
04

Electric Potential Energy and Electric Potential

Just as gravity creates gravitational potential energy, conservative electrostatic forces give rise to electric potential energy and electric potential (voltage).

  • Electric Potential Energy (U): The work required to bring a charge from infinity to a point in an electric field. For two point charges: Potential Energy = Coulomb Constant × ((Charge 1 × Charge 2) / Distance) (U = ke × (q1 × q2) / r).
  • Electric Potential (V): Electric potential energy per unit charge, measured in Volts (V):
    Electric Potential = Potential Energy / Test Charge (V = U / q0).
  • Potential of a Point Charge:
    Electric Potential = Coulomb Constant × (Charge / Distance) (V = ke × q / r).
05

Capacitors and Capacitance

A capacitor is an electronic component designed to store electrical energy in an electrostatic field. It typically consists of two parallel conducting plates separated by a vacuum or insulating dielectric material.

  • Definition of Capacitance (C): The ratio of the magnitude of electric charge (Q) on either plate to the potential difference (V) between the plates:
    Capacitance = Stored Charge / Potential Difference (C = Q / V), measured in Farads (F).
  • Parallel-Plate Capacitor: For plates of area A separated by distance d in a vacuum:
    Capacitance = Permittivity of Free Space × Area / Distance (C = epsilon0 × A / d).
  • Energy Stored in a Capacitor:
    Energy Stored = (1/2) × Capacitance × Potential Difference squared (U = (1/2) × C × V2).

Exhaustive Worked Exam Examples

Problem 1 (Coulomb's Law Calculation):

Calculate the electrostatic force between two small point charges of q1 = +3.00 × 10-6 C and q2 = -5.00 × 10-6 C separated by a distance of r = 0.500 m in a vacuum. (Use ke = 8.99 × 109 N·m2/C2).

Detailed Step-by-Step Solution:
- Coulomb's Law Formula: F = ke × (|q1| × |q2|) / r2.
- Substitute values:
F = (8.99 × 109) × ((3.00 × 10-6) × (5.00 × 10-6)) / (0.500)2.
- Numerator: (8.99 × 109) × (1.50 × 10-11) = 0.13485 N·m2.
- Denominator: (0.500 m)2 = 0.250 m2.
- Division: F = 0.13485 / 0.250 = 0.539 Newtons (attractive force).
Problem 2 (Electric Field of a Point Charge):

Calculate the magnitude and direction of the electric field at a point located 0.200 m away from a positive point charge of q = +4.00 × 10-9 C. (Use ke = 8.99 × 109 N·m2/C2).

Detailed Step-by-Step Solution:
- Electric Field Formula: E = ke × |q| / r2.
- Substitute values:
E = (8.99 × 109 N·m2/C2) × (4.00 × 10-9 C) / (0.200 m)2.
- Numerator: 8.99 × 4.00 = 35.96 N·m2/C.
- Denominator: (0.200)2 = 0.0400 m2.
- Division: E = 35.96 / 0.0400 = 899 N/C (directed radially outward away from the positive charge).
Problem 3 (Parallel-Plate Capacitor Energy Storage):

A parallel-plate capacitor has a capacitance of C = 25.0 microfarads (2.50 × 10-5 F) and is connected across a 12.0 V battery until fully charged. Calculate: (a) the stored charge on the plates, and (b) the total electrostatic energy stored in the capacitor.

Detailed Step-by-Step Solution:
(a) Stored Charge (Q):
- Formula: Q = C × V.
- Q = (2.50 × 10-5 F) × (12.0 V) = 3.00 × 10-4 Coulombs (0.300 mC).

(b) Stored Energy (U):
- Formula: U = (1/2) × C × V2.
- U = (1/2) × (2.50 × 10-5 F) × (12.0 V)2 = (1/2) × (2.50 × 10-5) × 144 = 1.80 × 10-3 Joules = 1.80 mJ.
