Introduction to Gravitation & Space Mechanics

Gravitation is the universal attractive interaction existing between all matter in the universe. While terrestrial gravity near the surface of the Earth imparts a nearly constant downward acceleration (g approximately 9.81 m/s2), Isaac Newton recognized that the exact same force governs the orbits of the Moon around the Earth, artificial communication satellites, interplanetary space probes, and ballistic rocket trajectories.

Core Conceptual Principle:
Gravity is an attractive central force that acts along the line connecting the centers of two interacting masses. It is the foundational force governing celestial mechanics, orbital maneuvers, and space exploration.
02

Newton's Law of Universal Gravitation

Newton's Law of Universal Gravitation states that every point mass attracts every other point mass with a force proportional to the product of their masses and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between them:

Equation:
Fg = (G * m1 * m2) / r2
where G is the universal gravitational constant (G approximately 6.674 × 10-11 N·m2/kg2), m1 and m2 are the interacting masses, and r is the center-to-center separation distance.
  • Inverse-Square Nature: Because force falls off as 1/r2, doubling the distance between two masses reduces the gravitational attraction by a factor of four.
  • Shell Theorem: A spherically symmetric mass distributes its gravitational force outside its radius as if all its mass were concentrated at its exact geometric center.
03

Gravitational Field Strength

The gravitational field vector (g) at any point in space is defined as the gravitational force experienced by a small test mass mtest placed at that point, divided by the test mass:

  • Definition: g = Fg / mtest
  • Magnitude for a Spherically Symmetric Mass M: g = (G * M) / r2
    measured in Newtons per kilogram (N/kg) or meters per second squared (m/s2).
  • Altitude Variation: As an object moves to an altitude h above the Earth's surface (where r = RE + h, with RE being the Earth's radius), gravitational field strength decreases according to:
    g(h) = (G * ME) / (RE + h)2
04

Gravitational Potential Energy (General)

Near the Earth's surface, gravitational potential energy is approximated as U = mgh. However, for large astronomical distances where g varies, we use the general expression derived by integrating the universal gravitational force from infinity (r = infinity, where potential energy is defined as zero) to a distance r:

  • General Potential Energy Formula: U(r) = - ((G * M * m) / r)
  • Negative Sign Significance: Gravitational potential energy is always negative because zero potential energy is defined at infinite separation (r = infinity), and work must be done against gravity to separate masses.
05

Kepler's Planetary Laws

Johannes Kepler formulated three empirical laws describing planetary motion, which Isaac Newton later proved were exact mathematical consequences of his Law of Gravitation:

  • First Law (Law of Orbits): All planets and satellites move in elliptical orbits, with the central primary body located at one of the two focal points.
  • Second Law (Law of Equal Areas): A line segment joining a planet or satellite to the central body sweeps out equal areas during equal intervals of time. This is a direct consequence of the conservation of angular momentum (L = m * v * r * sin(theta) = constant), meaning orbiting bodies move fastest when closest to the primary body (periapsis) and slowest when farthest (apoapsis).
  • Third Law (Law of Periods): The square of the orbital period (T) of any orbiting body is directly proportional to the cube of the semi-major axis (r) of its orbit:
    T2 = ((4 * pi2) / (G * M)) * r3
06

Satellite Orbital Mechanics

A satellite of mass m orbiting a central astronomical body of mass M at a stable orbital radius r experiences a centripetal force provided entirely by the gravitational attraction:

  • Orbital Speed (v): Equating gravitational force to centripetal force ((G * M * m) / r2 = (m * v2) / r):
    v = square root of ((G * M) / r)
  • Orbital Period (T): The time required for one complete orbit (T = (2 * pi * r) / v):
    T = 2 * pi * square root of (r3 / (G * M))
  • Geostationary Satellites: A satellite whose orbital period matches the rotational period of the Earth (24 hours), remaining permanently fixed above a single geographic location on the equator at an altitude of approximately 35,786 km.
  • Total Orbital Energy: E = K + U = (1/2)*m*v2 - ((G * M * m) / r) = - ((G * M * m) / (2 * r)).
07

Rocket Propulsion & Tsiolkovsky Mechanics

Rocket propulsion operates entirely on the conservation of momentum (Newton's Third Law). As a rocket expels exhaust gases backward at high velocity relative to the rocket, the rocket gains forward momentum.

  • Tsiolkovsky Rocket Equation: Relates the final velocity achieved by a rocket to its initial mass (mi including propellant), final dry mass (mf after all propellant is burned), and effective exhaust velocity (ve):
    delta v = ve * ln(mi / mf)
  • Mass Ratio: The efficiency and capability of a rocket depend heavily on its mass ratio (mi / mf). Because most of a launch vehicle's mass consists of chemical propellant, multi-stage rockets are utilized to drop empty fuel tanks and increase payload delivery capability.
  • Thrust Force: Fthrust = vrel * (dm / dt), where dm/dt is the mass burn rate of the expelled exhaust gas.
08

Gravitational Escape Velocity

Escape velocity (vesc) is the minimum initial speed required for an unpowered object (such as a spacecraft or rocket) to escape the gravitational field of a celestial body and reach infinite distance with zero residual speed.

Derivation via Energy Conservation (Ki + Ui = Kf + Uf):
Setting Kf = 0 and Uf = 0 at r = infinity:
(1/2) * m * vesc2 - ((G * M * m) / R) = 0 implies vesc = square root of ((2 * G * M) / R)
where R is the radius of the celestial body. Note that escape velocity is independent of the escaping object's mass (m). For Earth, the escape speed from the surface is approximately 11.2 km/s.

Exhaustive Worked Exam Examples

Problem 1 (Satellite Orbital Speed & Period):

A communications satellite is placed in a circular orbit at an altitude of h = 35,786 km above the Earth's surface. Given that the Earth's mass is ME = 5.972 × 1024 kg and its mean radius is RE = 6.371 × 106 m, calculate: (a) the orbital speed of the satellite, and (b) its orbital period in hours. (Use G = 6.674 × 10-11 N·m2/kg2).

Detailed Step-by-Step Solution:
(a) Orbital Radius and Speed (v):
- Total orbital radius r = RE + h = 6.371 × 106 m + 35.786 × 106 m = 4.2157 × 107 m.
- v = square root of ((G * ME) / r) = square root of (((6.674 × 10-11)(5.972 × 1024)) / (4.2157 × 107)) = square root of (3.986 × 1014 / 4.2157 × 107) = square root of (9.455 × 106) approximately equal to 3075 m/s (3.08 km/s).

(b) Orbital Period (T):
- T = (2 * pi * r) / v = (2 * pi * 4.2157 × 107 m) / (3075 m/s) = (2.648 × 108) / 3075 approximately equal to 86,114 seconds.
- Convert to hours: T = 86,114 / 3600 approximately equal to 23.9 hours (a geostationary orbit!).
Problem 2 (Escape Speed Calculation):

Calculate the escape speed from the surface of the Moon. Given that the Moon's mass is MM = 7.342 × 1022 kg and its radius is RM = 1.737 × 106 m. (Use G = 6.674 × 10-11 N·m2/kg2).

Detailed Step-by-Step Solution:
- Formula for escape speed: vesc = square root of ((2 * G * MM) / RM).
- Substitute values:
vesc = square root of (((2 * 6.674 × 10-11 N·m2/kg2) * (7.342 × 1022 kg)) / (1.737 × 106 m)).
- vesc = square root of (9.791 × 1012 / 1.737 × 106) = square root of (5.637 × 106) approximately equal to 2374 m/s (2.37 km/s).
Problem 3 (Rocket Delta-V via Tsiolkovsky Equation):

A sounding rocket has an effective exhaust velocity of ve = 2500 m/s. Its initial total mass is mi = 10,000 kg, of which 8,000 kg is fuel (leaving a dry mass of mf = 2,000 kg). Calculate the maximum velocity change (delta v) delivered by the rocket engine in free space (ignoring gravity and atmospheric drag).

Detailed Step-by-Step Solution:
- Tsiolkovsky Rocket Equation: delta v = ve * ln(mi / mf).
- Mass ratio: mi / mf = 10,000 kg / 2,000 kg = 5.0.
- Natural logarithm: ln(5.0) approximately equal to 1.609.
- delta v = (2500 m/s) * 1.609 approximately equal to 4023 m/s (4.02 km/s).
