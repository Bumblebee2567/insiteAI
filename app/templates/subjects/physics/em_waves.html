Introduction to Electromagnetic Waves

Electromagnetic (EM) waves are transverse waves consisting of oscillating electric and magnetic fields that propagate through space at the speed of light. Unlike mechanical waves, electromagnetic waves require no physical medium to travel; they can propagate perfectly through a complete vacuum (such as the empty space between the Sun and Earth).

Core Conceptual Principle:
A changing electric field creates a magnetic field, and a changing magnetic field creates an electric field. This mutual induction allows electromagnetic fields to sustain themselves and travel infinitely through empty space.
02

Overview of Maxwell's Equations

Formulated by James Clerk Maxwell in the 19th century, Maxwell's four equations form the complete theoretical foundation of classical electromagnetism:

  • Gauss's Law for Electricity: Electric field lines originate on positive charges and terminate on negative charges (Net electric flux through a closed surface is proportional to enclosed electric charge).
  • Gauss's Law for Magnetism: Magnetic monopoles do not exist; magnetic field lines are always continuous closed loops (Net magnetic flux through any closed surface is always zero).
  • Faraday's Law of Induction: A time-varying magnetic field induces a circulating electric field.
  • Ampère-Maxwell Law: Magnetic fields are generated both by conduction electric currents and by time-varying electric displacement fields.
03

Properties and Propagation of EM Waves

In an electromagnetic wave traveling through a vacuum, the electric field vector (E) and magnetic field vector (B) are perpendicular to each other and both are perpendicular to the direction of wave propagation (transverse wave).

  • Speed of Light in Vacuum (c): Derived directly from Maxwell's equations using the permittivity (epsilon0) and permeability (mu0) of free space:
    Speed of Light = 1 / square root of (Permittivity of Free Space × Permeability of Free Space)
    (c = 1 / sqrt(epsilon0 * mu0) ≈ 3.00 × 108 m/s).
  • Field Magnitude Ratio: At any point along the wave, the ratio of the electric field magnitude to the magnetic field magnitude equals the speed of light:
    Electric Field Magnitude / Magnetic Field Magnitude = Speed of Light (E / B = c).

The Electromagnetic Spectrum

Electromagnetic waves span an enormous continuum of frequencies and wavelengths, known as the electromagnetic spectrum. All EM waves travel at the exact same speed in a vacuum (c), obeying the wave equation: Speed of Light = Frequency × Wavelength (c = f * lambda).

05

Energy Density, Intensity, and Radiation Pressure

Electromagnetic waves transport both energy and momentum through space. The energy in an EM wave is shared equally between its electric and magnetic fields.

  • Total Electromagnetic Energy Density (u):
    Energy Density = Permittivity of Free Space × Electric Field squared (u = epsilon0 * E2), measured in Joules per cubic meter (J/m3).
  • Intensity (I): The rate of energy flow per unit area (Poynting vector magnitude averaged over time):
    Intensity = (Speed of Light × Permittivity of Free Space × Peak Electric Field squared) / 2 (I = (c * epsilon0 * Emax2) / 2).
  • Radiation Pressure: When EM waves strike an absorbing surface, they exert a microscopic mechanical force known as radiation pressure:
    Pressure = Intensity / Speed of Light (Prad = I / c).

Exhaustive Worked Exam Examples

Problem 1 (Frequency and Wavelength Calculation):

A microwave oven operates at a frequency of f = 2.45 GHz (2.45 × 109 Hz). Given that the speed of light in a vacuum is c = 3.00 × 108 m/s, calculate the wavelength of this microwave radiation in meters and centimeters.

Detailed Step-by-Step Solution:
- Wave Equation: c = f * lambda implies lambda = c / f.
- Substitute values:
lambda = (3.00 × 108 m/s) / (2.45 × 109 Hz).
- Division: 3.00 / 2.45 = 1.2244.
- Exponent: 108 / 109 = 10-1.
- lambda = 0.1224 meters = 12.2 centimeters.
Problem 2 (Magnetic Field Amplitude from Electric Field):

An electromagnetic plane wave traveling through empty space has a peak electric field magnitude of Emax = 150 V/m. Calculate the peak magnetic field magnitude (Bmax) of the wave.

Detailed Step-by-Step Solution:
- Relationship between field peaks: Emax / Bmax = c implies Bmax = Emax / c.
- Substitute values:
Bmax = (150 V/m) / (3.00 × 108 m/s).
- Division: 150 / 3.00 × 108 = 50 × 10-8 = 5.00 × 10-7 Tesla = 0.500 microteslas.
Problem 3 (Energy Density Calculation):

Calculate the total electromagnetic energy density (u) stored in a region of space where the peak electric field strength of a light wave is E = 300 V/m. (Given permittivity of free space epsilon0 = 8.854 × 10-12 C2/(N·m2)).

Detailed Step-by-Step Solution:
- Energy Density Formula: u = epsilon0 * E2.
- Substitute values:
u = (8.854 × 10-12 C2/(N·m2)) × (300 V/m)2.
- Square the electric field: (300)2 = 9.00 × 104 V2/m2.
- Multiplication: (8.854 × 10-12) × (9.00 × 104) = 79.686 × 10-8 = 7.97 × 10-7 Joules/m3 = 0.797 microjoules per cubic meter.
