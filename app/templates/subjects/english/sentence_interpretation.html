01

Comprehensive Introduction to Sentence Interpretation

In West African examinations like JAMB and WAEC, sentence interpretation questions test a candidate's ability to grasp the core essence, underlying logic, and precise semantic implications of statements without adding outside assumptions.

Candidates are frequently given a statement followed by four multiple-choice options. The objective is to select the option that retains the exact core message while rephrasing or decoding the implicit meaning.

Core Exam Principle:
Never select an option simply because it sounds sophisticated or mentions true facts. The correct option must be logically and semantically derived directly from the provided sentence.
02

Closest in Meaning (Paraphrasing)

Paraphrasing questions test whether you can recognize when two sentences convey the exact same message using different vocabulary and syntax.

  • Qualifying Modifiers: Pay close attention to words like hardly, scarcely, seldom, and rarely. They render a sentence negative in meaning.
  • Active vs. Passive Voice: Ensure that changing the voice in the options does not alter the original actor or receiver of the action.
03

Decoding Implied Statements

An implied statement is a conclusion that can be drawn legitimately from a premise without being explicitly stated word-for-word.

Example Analysis:
Statement: "Had the driver applied his brakes sooner, the collision would have been averted."
Implied Fact: The driver failed to apply his brakes in time, resulting in the collision.
04

Logical Deduction & Conditional Truths

Candidates must evaluate conditional statements and negative premises carefully to avoid logical fallacies. Remember that proving the opposite of a condition does not automatically prove the converse unless explicitly stated.

05

Confusing Examination Traps & Distractors

Examiners use specific distractor patterns to mislead candidates.

Trap 1: Partial Truths

Options that capture one clause of a compound sentence while ignoring or contradicting the second clause.

Trap 2: Unsupported Generalizations

Options that expand a specific statement into a sweeping general rule not warranted by the text.

07

Exhaustive Worked Exam Examples

Problem 1 (Interpreting Negative Correlatives):

What is the logical implication of:
"Scarcely had the match commenced when a heavy downpour disrupted proceedings."

Detailed Step-by-Step Solution:
1. Analyze Structure: "Scarcely... when" indicates two events happening almost simultaneously.
2. Logical Deduction: The rain started immediately after the match began, interrupting it almost at inception.
