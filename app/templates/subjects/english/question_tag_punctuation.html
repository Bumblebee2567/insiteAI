01

Comprehensive Introduction to Tags & Mechanics

Precision in written and spoken English requires not only advanced grammar and vocabulary but also flawless mechanics. In West African examinations such as JAMB and WAEC, questions testing question tags and punctuation require strict adherence to structural rules.

A question tag is a short auxiliary-plus-pronoun construction appended to the end of a statement to turn it into a question or seek confirmation. Punctuation marks, by extension, act as the structural road signs of writing, controlling breath, cadence, and meaning.

Core Structural Principle:
• Polarity Rule: A positive statement requires a negative question tag, whereas a negative statement requires a positive question tag.
• Pronoun Rule: Question tags must always use pronoun forms corresponding to the subject, never full nouns.
02

Mastering Question Tag Formation

Constructing a correct question tag requires identifying the tense, auxiliary verb, and polarity of the main clause.

Main Statement Statement Polarity Correct Question Tag Explanation & Rule
She is arriving today, ... Positive isn't she? Positive auxiliary 'is' becomes negative 'isn\'t' with pronoun 'she'.
They do not like rice, ... Negative do they? Negative auxiliary 'do not' becomes positive 'do' with pronoun 'they'.
Chinedu passed the test, ... Positive (Past) didn't he? Implied past tense auxiliary 'did' becomes negative 'didn\'t' with 'he'.
03

Special Question Tag Exceptions

Examiners frequently test irregular question tag exceptions that violate standard polarity rules.

  • First Person Singular (I am): The tag for "I am" is aren't I? (Never use amn't I?).
    Example: I am invited to the seminar, aren't I?
  • Imperatives (Commands/Requests): Imperative statements take will you? or won't you?
    Example: Shut the door, will you?
  • Suggestions with "Let us": Sentences beginning with "Let us" take shall we?
    Example: Let us review the notes, shall we?
04

Essential Punctuation Mechanics

Mastering semicolons, colons, and apostrophes is vital for objective mechanics test items.

  • Semicolon (;): Links two closely related independent clauses without a coordinating conjunction.
  • Colon (:): Introduces a list, explanation, or formal quotation after an independent clause.
  • Apostrophe (\x27): Used for possession (e.g., boy's, boys') and contractions, but never for possessive pronouns like its, ours, or yours.
05

Confusing Examination Traps & Distractors

Examiners use clever distractors to test mechanical exceptions.

Trap 1: Semi-Negative Words

Words like seldom, rarely, hardly, scarcely, and little are grammatically negative. Therefore, they require a positive question tag.
• Correct: He rarely visits his hometown, does he? (Not doesn't he).

Trap 2: Possessive Pronoun Apostrophes

Confusing contractions with possessive pronouns (e.g., writing it's when intending the possessive pronoun its).
