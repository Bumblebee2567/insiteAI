What Are Polymers?

A polymer is a very large molecule formed from many smaller repeating molecules called monomers. The word polymer comes from the idea of "many units".

Monomer + Monomer + Monomer + ⋯  →  Polymer A polymer contains many repeating structural units.

Monomer

A small molecule capable of joining with other molecules to form a polymer.

Polymer

A giant molecule containing many repeating monomer units.

Repeating Unit

The structural unit that repeats throughout the polymer chain.

02

Monomers and Repeating Units

The identity and structure of a monomer determine the structure and properties of the polymer formed from it.

Ethene

CH₂=CH₂ is an important monomer used to manufacture poly(ethene).

CH2=CH2

Propene

CH₂=CHCH₃ can undergo addition polymerisation to form poly(propene).

CH2=CHCH3
Key idea: In addition polymerisation, the double bond in an alkene provides the reactive site that allows monomers to join together.
03

Polymerisation

Polymerisation is the chemical process in which monomers join together to form a polymer.

Addition Polymerisation

Unsaturated monomers, usually alkenes, join together without producing a small molecule as a by-product.

Condensation Polymerisation

Monomers containing suitable functional groups join together while a small molecule such as water is eliminated.

04

Addition Polymerisation

In addition polymerisation, many alkene molecules react together. The carbon-carbon double bond opens and new carbon-carbon single bonds connect the monomers into a long chain.

Monomer

CH2=CH2

Ethene

Polymer

[−CH2−CH2−]n

Poly(ethene)

nCH2=CH2  →  [−CH2−CH2−]n The double bond opens as the polymer chain forms.
05

Condensation Polymerisation

Condensation polymerisation involves monomers with two or more reactive functional groups. When the monomers join, a small molecule such as water is eliminated.

General pattern:
Monomer + Monomer  →  Larger molecule + Small molecule

Common small molecules released include H₂O and HCl.

Polyesters

Formed from monomers containing alcohol and carboxylic-acid functional groups.

Polyamides

Formed using monomers capable of creating amide links between repeating units.

Polymer Calculations

Polymer questions can involve the number of repeating units, relative molecular mass, and degree of polymerisation.

Worked Example — Degree of Polymerisation

A polymer has an average relative molecular mass of 28,000. The repeating unit has a relative molecular mass of 28. Calculate the number of repeating units.

1
Use: Degree of polymerisation = polymer Mr ÷ repeating-unit Mr
2
Substitute: 28,000 ÷ 28
Degree of polymerisation = 1000 repeating units
n = Mpolymer / Mrepeat n represents the approximate number of repeating units.
09

Environmental Impact of Polymers

Recycling

Recycling allows some polymer materials to be processed and used again, reducing demand for new raw materials.

Waste

Many synthetic polymers degrade slowly and can persist in the environment for long periods.

Alternatives

Biodegradable and renewable polymer materials can provide alternatives for selected applications.
