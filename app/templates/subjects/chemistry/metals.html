Properties of Metals

Metals are generally strong, shiny materials that conduct heat and electricity because their outer electrons are delocalised throughout the metallic structure.

Electrical Conductivity

Delocalised electrons can move through the metallic lattice and carry electrical charge.

Thermal Conductivity

Energy is transferred efficiently through the closely packed metallic structure.

Malleability

Layers of metal ions can slide while remaining attracted to the delocalised electrons.

Key idea: Metallic bonding consists of positive metal ions surrounded by a sea of delocalised electrons.

Reactions with Water

Different metals react with water at different rates, depending on their position in the reactivity series.

Potassium

Reacts extremely vigorously with cold water, producing potassium hydroxide and hydrogen.

Calcium

Reacts with cold water less violently and produces calcium hydroxide and hydrogen.

Magnesium

Reacts slowly with cold water but much more readily with steam.

2Na + 2H₂O → 2NaOH + H₂
05

Reactions with Acids

Metals above hydrogen in the reactivity series can react with dilute acids to produce a salt and hydrogen gas.

Metal + Acid → Salt + Hydrogen

Worked Example — Magnesium + Hydrochloric Acid

1
Write the balanced equation:
Mg + 2HCl → MgCl₂ + H₂
2
One mole of magnesium produces one mole of hydrogen gas.
3
Therefore, if 0.20 mol Mg reacts completely:
n(H₂) = 0.20 mol
Answer: 0.20 mol of hydrogen is produced.
06

Extraction of Metals

The method used to extract a metal depends largely on its position in the reactivity series.

Reduction with Carbon

Metals below carbon can often be extracted by heating their ores with carbon or carbon monoxide.

Electrolysis

Very reactive metals such as aluminium are extracted from molten compounds using electrolysis.

Ore Processing

Ores are processed and concentrated before the metal is separated from its compounds.

Rule: Metals more reactive than carbon generally cannot be extracted by simple reduction with carbon.
07

Electrolysis

Electrolysis uses electrical energy to drive a chemical change that would not occur spontaneously.

Cathode

Positive metal ions gain electrons at the cathode. This is reduction.

Anode

Negative ions lose electrons at the anode. This is oxidation.

Al³⁺ + 3e⁻ → Al Aluminium ions are reduced at the cathode.

Alloys

An alloy is a mixture containing a metal and one or more other elements. Different-sized atoms can disrupt the regular metallic layers.

Alloy Main components Common use

Stainless steel Iron + Chromium Kitchen equipment
Brass Copper + Zinc Musical instruments and fittings
Bronze Copper + Tin Statues and engineering
09

Corrosion

Corrosion is the gradual destruction of a metal through chemical reactions with its environment.

Rusting of iron requires:
Iron + Oxygen + Water → Rust

Rust is a hydrated form of iron(III) oxide.
10

Preventing Corrosion

Painting

Creates a barrier that prevents oxygen and water from reaching the metal.

Galvanising

Zinc coating protects iron from corrosion and can provide sacrificial protection.

Alloying

Metals can be combined with other elements to produce corrosion-resistant materials.

  • Know that rusting requires both water and oxygen.
