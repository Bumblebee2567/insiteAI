More Chemistry Concepts

A broad high-school chemistry guide covering matter, atomic structure, periodicity, bonding, reactions, acids and bases, stoichiometry, energy, rates, equilibrium, organic chemistry, electrochemistry and other major areas of chemistry.

Matter Atomic Structure Periodic Table Bonding Formulae Reactions Moles Acids & Bases Solutions Energy Rates Equilibrium Redox Electrochemistry Organic Chemistry Environmental Chemistry Word Problems

1. Matter and Its Properties

Matter is anything that has mass and occupies space. Everything around us is made of matter, from the air we breathe to the water we drink and the materials used to build houses, phones and machines.

States of Matter

Solid

Particles are closely packed and mainly vibrate around fixed positions. Solids have a definite shape and volume.

Liquid

Particles remain relatively close together but can move past one another. Liquids have a definite volume but take the shape of their container.

Gas

Particles are widely separated and move freely. Gases have neither a fixed shape nor a fixed volume.

Plasma

Plasma is an ionized state of matter containing charged particles. It occurs in phenomena such as stars and electrical discharges.

Physical and Chemical Changes

A physical change does not produce a new substance. A chemical change results in the formation of one or more new substances.

Physical change: melting ice.

Chemical change: iron reacting with oxygen to form rust.

2. Atomic Structure

An atom is the basic unit of an element that retains the chemical identity of that element.

Subatomic Particles

Particle Charge Relative Mass Location
Proton +1 1 Nucleus
Neutron 0 1 Nucleus
Electron -1 Approximately 1/1836 Outside nucleus

Atomic Number

The atomic number is the number of protons in the nucleus of an atom. It identifies the element.

Atomic number = number of protons

Mass Number

Mass number = protons + neutrons
Example: Carbon-12 contains 6 protons and 6 neutrons.

Isotopes

Isotopes are atoms of the same element that have the same number of protons but different numbers of neutrons.

3. Periodic Table and Periodicity

The periodic table arranges elements according to increasing atomic number and groups elements with related chemical properties.

Groups and Periods

  • A vertical column is called a group.
  • A horizontal row is called a period.
  • Elements in the same group often have similar chemical properties because they have related outer-electron configurations.

Important Groups

Group 1

Alkali metals. They are generally very reactive metals and commonly form +1 ions.

Group 2

Alkaline earth metals. They commonly form +2 ions.

Group 17

Halogens. Reactive non-metals that commonly form -1 ions.

Group 18

Noble gases. They have very low chemical reactivity under ordinary conditions.

Periodic Trends

Important periodic trends include atomic radius, ionic radius, ionization energy, electron affinity and electronegativity.

General trend: Atomic radius generally decreases across a period and increases down a group.

4. Chemical Bonding

Chemical bonding describes the attractive forces that hold atoms or ions together in substances.

Ionic Bonding

Ionic bonding involves electrostatic attraction between oppositely charged ions. It commonly occurs between metals and non-metals.

Na → Na⁺ + e⁻

Cl + e⁻ → Cl⁻

Na⁺ + Cl⁻ → NaCl

Covalent Bonding

Covalent bonding involves sharing electron pairs between atoms.

H₂     O₂     N₂     CH₄

Metallic Bonding

Metallic bonding involves positive metal ions surrounded by delocalized electrons. This helps explain the electrical and thermal conductivity of metals.

5. Chemical Formulae and Equations

Chemical formulae communicate the composition of substances, while chemical equations represent chemical reactions.

Examples

H₂O
CO₂
CaCO₃

Balancing Equations

Chemical equations are balanced because atoms are conserved during chemical reactions.

2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O
Remember: Change coefficients when balancing equations. Do not change the subscripts of chemical formulae.

6. Chemical Reactions

Chemical reactions involve atoms or ions being rearranged to form new substances.

Common Reaction Types

Synthesis

A + B → AB

Decomposition

AB → A + B

Single Replacement

A + BC → AC + B

Double Replacement

AB + CD → AD + CB

Combustion

Hydrocarbons commonly burn in oxygen to produce carbon dioxide and water when complete combustion occurs.

CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O

7. The Mole and Stoichiometry

The mole is the fundamental chemical unit used to count extremely large numbers of particles.

n = m / M

where n is amount in moles, m is mass and M is molar mass.

Avogadro's Constant

Nₐ ≈ 6.022 × 10²³ mol⁻¹

One mole contains approximately 6.022 × 10²³ specified particles.

Stoichiometry

Stoichiometry uses the coefficients in balanced chemical equations to calculate quantities of reactants and products.

Example

Consider:

2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O

Two moles of hydrogen react with one mole of oxygen to produce two moles of water.

8. Acids, Bases and Salts

Acids

Acids are substances that can donate hydrogen ions in appropriate chemical contexts. Common acids include hydrochloric acid, sulfuric acid and nitric acid.

Bases

Bases react with acids and many soluble bases are called alkalis.

Neutralisation

An acid and a base can react to produce salt and water.

Acid + Base → Salt + Water

Example

HCl + NaOH → NaCl + H₂O

pH

The pH scale is used to describe the acidity or basicity of aqueous solutions.

pH = -log₁₀[H⁺]
Generally: Lower pH means greater acidity, while higher pH indicates a more basic solution.

9. Solutions and Concentration

A solution is a homogeneous mixture in which a solute is dissolved in a solvent.

Concentration

Concentration = amount of solute / volume of solution

A common chemistry unit is mol dm⁻³.

Example

If 0.50 mol of solute is dissolved to make 2.0 dm³ of solution:

c = 0.50 / 2.0
c = 0.25 mol dm⁻³

Solubility

Solubility describes how much of a substance can dissolve in a particular solvent under specified conditions.

10. Energy Changes in Chemical Reactions

Chemical reactions involve energy changes. Some reactions release energy while others absorb energy.

Exothermic Reactions

Exothermic reactions release energy to the surroundings.

Examples: combustion and many neutralisation reactions.

Endothermic Reactions

Endothermic reactions absorb energy from the surroundings.

Heat Equation

q = mcΔT

where q is heat energy, m is mass, c is specific heat capacity and ΔT is temperature change.

Enthalpy Change

ΔH = H(products) - H(reactants)

11. Rate of Chemical Reaction

Reaction rate describes how quickly reactants are converted into products.

Rate = change in quantity / time

Factors Affecting Rate

  • Temperature
  • Concentration
  • Pressure for gases
  • Surface area
  • Catalysts

Collision Theory

For a reaction to occur, particles must collide with sufficient energy and with a suitable orientation.

Activation Energy

Activation energy is the minimum energy required for a successful reaction to occur.

12. Chemical Equilibrium

In a reversible reaction, equilibrium occurs when the forward and reverse reactions continue at equal rates.

A + B ⇌ C + D

Le Chatelier's Principle

When a system at equilibrium is disturbed, the system responds in a way that tends to oppose the disturbance.

Factors

  • Concentration
  • Temperature
  • Pressure in gaseous systems
Important: A catalyst can help a system reach equilibrium faster but does not change the equilibrium position.

13. Oxidation and Reduction

Redox reactions involve transfer of electrons or changes in oxidation state.

Oxidation

Loss of electrons.

OIL: Oxidation Is Loss.

Reduction

Gain of electrons.

RIG: Reduction Is Gain.

Zn → Zn²⁺ + 2e⁻

Zinc has lost electrons, so zinc has been oxidised.

14. Electrochemistry

Electrochemistry studies the relationship between chemical reactions and electrical energy.

Electrolysis

Electrolysis uses electrical energy to drive a chemical change that would not occur spontaneously under the same conditions.

Electrodes

  • Oxidation occurs at the anode.
  • Reduction occurs at the cathode.
Memory aid: AN OX, RED CAT — oxidation at the anode, reduction at the cathode.

Electrochemical Cells

Galvanic or voltaic cells generate electrical energy from spontaneous redox reactions.

15. Organic Chemistry

Organic chemistry focuses primarily on carbon-containing compounds.

Hydrocarbons

Hydrocarbons contain only carbon and hydrogen.

Alkanes

Saturated hydrocarbons containing only single carbon-carbon bonds.

CₙH₂ₙ₊₂

Alkenes

Unsaturated hydrocarbons containing at least one carbon- carbon double bond.

CₙH₂ₙ

Common Functional Groups

Family Functional Group Example
Alcohol -OH Ethanol
Carboxylic acid -COOH Ethanoic acid
Alkene C=C Ethene

Polymerisation

Polymerisation is the process in which many small molecules called monomers join together to form a polymer.

16. Environmental Chemistry

Chemistry helps us understand atmospheric pollution, water treatment, climate processes and the environmental effects of industrial activity.

Major Environmental Issues

  • Greenhouse gases
  • Acid rain
  • Air pollution
  • Water pollution
  • Ozone depletion
  • Plastic pollution
  • Industrial waste

Greenhouse Effect

Certain gases absorb and re-emit infrared radiation, contributing to warming of Earth's atmosphere.

Acid Rain

Sulfur and nitrogen oxides released into the atmosphere can contribute to the formation of acidic precipitation.

17. Chemistry Word Problems

Chemistry problems often combine several ideas. Read the question carefully, identify the known quantities, select the appropriate formula and keep track of units.

Problem 1 — Moles

A student has 36 g of water. Calculate the number of moles present.

Relative formula mass of H₂O = 18.

n = m / M
n = 36 / 18
Answer: 2 mol

Problem 2 — Concentration

0.20 mol of sodium hydroxide is dissolved to produce 500 cm³ of solution. Calculate the concentration in mol dm⁻³.

Convert:

500 cm³ = 0.500 dm³
c = n / V
c = 0.20 / 0.500
Answer: 0.40 mol dm⁻³

Problem 3 — Heat Energy

A 100 g sample of water is heated through 10°C. If the specific heat capacity of water is 4.18 J g⁻¹ °C⁻¹, calculate the heat absorbed.

q = mcΔT
q = 100 × 4.18 × 10
Answer: 4180 J

Problem 4 — Chemical Equation

Methane burns completely in oxygen. Write the balanced chemical equation.

CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O
The equation is balanced because the number of each type of atom is the same on both sides.

Problem 5 — Stoichiometry

Consider the reaction:

2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O

If 4 mol of hydrogen react completely with excess oxygen, how many moles of water can be produced?

The mole ratio is:

2 mol H₂ : 2 mol H₂O
4 mol H₂ produces 4 mol H₂O.
