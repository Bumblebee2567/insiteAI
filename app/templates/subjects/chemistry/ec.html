What Is Environmental Chemistry?

Environmental chemistry studies the chemical substances, reactions and processes occurring in the atmosphere, hydrosphere, soil and living systems.

It helps us understand how natural processes and human activities alter the chemical composition of the environment and how those changes affect ecosystems, human health and climate.

Atmosphere

Chemistry of gases, aerosols, pollutants, ozone and greenhouse gases.

Hydrosphere

Chemistry of rivers, lakes, groundwater, oceans and drinking water.

Ecosystems

Chemical substances circulate between air, water, soil and living organisms.

Core idea: Environmental problems are often chemical problems involving concentration, reaction rates, equilibrium, oxidation-reduction, solubility and energy transfer.
02

Air Pollution

Air pollution occurs when harmful gases, particles or other substances are present in the atmosphere at concentrations capable of causing environmental or health effects.

Vehicle emissions

Combustion engines can release carbon monoxide, nitrogen oxides, hydrocarbons and particulate matter.

Industrial emissions

Industrial processes may release sulfur compounds, nitrogen oxides, particles and other pollutants.

Combustion

Burning fuels and biomass produces gases and particles whose composition depends on combustion conditions.

Particulate matter

Tiny solid and liquid particles can remain suspended in air and penetrate deeply into the respiratory system.

Major Air Pollutants

CO₂ Fossil-fuel combustion Important greenhouse gas.
SO₂ Burning sulfur-containing fuels Contributes to acid deposition and respiratory pollution.
NOₓ High-temperature combustion Contributes to acid deposition and photochemical smog.
Particulates Combustion, dust and industry Can damage respiratory and cardiovascular health.
VOCs Fuels, solvents and industry Can participate in photochemical reactions.

Primary vs secondary pollutants

A primary pollutant is emitted directly, while a secondary pollutant forms in the atmosphere through chemical reactions involving other substances.

04

Acid Rain & Acid Deposition

Sulfur dioxide and nitrogen oxides released into the atmosphere can undergo oxidation and react with water to form acidic substances.

SO₂ → SO₃ → H₂SO₄
Simplified pathway showing the formation of sulfuric acid.
NO₂ + H₂O → acids containing nitric acid
Atmospheric nitrogen oxides contribute to nitric acid formation.

Vegetation

Acid deposition can alter soil chemistry and affect nutrient availability.

Aquatic systems

Acidification can change the chemistry of lakes and streams and stress aquatic organisms.

Buildings

Acids can react with carbonate-containing stone and accelerate chemical weathering.

05

Water Pollution

Water pollution occurs when physical, chemical or biological contaminants enter water bodies and change their natural conditions.

Chemical pollutants

Heavy metals, acids, nutrients, pesticides, detergents and industrial chemicals.

Biological pollutants

Pathogenic microorganisms and excessive organic matter can make water unsafe.

Physical pollutants

Suspended solids, plastics, sediments and other materials can alter water quality.

Eutrophication

Excess nitrate and phosphate nutrients can stimulate excessive algae and plant growth. When this biomass decomposes, microorganisms consume dissolved oxygen, potentially producing low-oxygen conditions.

Key chain:
Excess nutrients → algal growth → decomposition → increased oxygen demand → reduced dissolved oxygen → stress or death of aquatic organisms.

Biochemical Oxygen Demand

BOD describes the amount of dissolved oxygen consumed by microorganisms while decomposing biodegradable organic matter in water.

06

Water Treatment Chemistry

Screening

Large debris and solids are physically removed.

Coagulation

Chemicals help destabilise small suspended particles so they can form larger aggregates.

Filtration

Water passes through a medium that removes remaining suspended material.

Disinfection

Chemical or physical methods reduce harmful microorganisms.

Chlorination

Chlorine-based disinfectants can produce chemically active species in water that damage microorganisms and help make treated water safer.

07

Greenhouse Gases

Greenhouse gases absorb and re-emit infrared radiation, contributing to the warming effect of Earth's atmosphere.

Carbon dioxide

Produced naturally and by processes including fossil-fuel combustion and land-use change.

Methane

A potent greenhouse gas associated with wetlands, agriculture, waste and fossil-fuel systems.

Nitrous oxide

Released by natural and agricultural processes and contributes to radiative forcing.

CO₂ + energy from combustion → CO₂ released to atmosphere
Carbon dioxide is not itself "pollution" in every context; its environmental significance depends strongly on concentration and atmospheric effects.
Important distinction: The greenhouse effect is a natural atmospheric process. Human activity can increase concentrations of greenhouse gases, strengthening the warming influence of the atmosphere.
08

Ozone Chemistry

Stratospheric ozone

Ozone in the stratosphere absorbs much of the Sun's harmful ultraviolet radiation.

Ground-level ozone

Ozone near Earth's surface is a secondary pollutant involved in photochemical smog.

Formation and breakdown

O₂ + UV → 2O
O + O₂ → O₃
Simplified stratospheric ozone formation pathway.

Ozone depletion

Certain halogen-containing compounds can release reactive chlorine or bromine species in the stratosphere. These species participate in catalytic cycles that destroy ozone.

Remember: Stratospheric ozone protects against ultraviolet radiation, while ground-level ozone is a harmful air pollutant.
09

Atmospheric & Climate Chemistry

Environmental chemistry connects atmospheric composition with chemical reactions, radiation, equilibrium and transport.

Photochemical smog

Sunlight can drive reactions between nitrogen oxides, volatile organic compounds and atmospheric oxygen, producing secondary pollutants including ozone.

Carbon cycle

Carbon continuously moves between the atmosphere, oceans, soils, rocks and living organisms through processes such as photosynthesis, respiration, decomposition and combustion.

Atmospheric chemistry = emissions + transport + reactions + removal A useful framework for analysing environmental chemical systems.

Chemical Solutions to Environmental Problems

Pollution control

Catalytic converters, filters, scrubbers and other technologies can reduce pollutant emissions.

Waste management

Recycling, controlled treatment and improved material recovery reduce environmental contamination.

Cleaner energy

Reducing combustion of carbon-intensive fuels can lower emissions of several atmospheric pollutants and greenhouse gases.

Water protection

Wastewater treatment and nutrient management reduce chemical loading in aquatic systems.

Green chemistry

Chemical processes can be redesigned to reduce hazardous substances, waste and energy consumption.

Monitoring

Chemical analysis allows pollutants and environmental conditions to be measured and tracked.

Environmental Chemistry Exam Checklist

Know these before the exam

  • Distinguish primary and secondary air pollutants.
  • Know major sources and effects of CO, CO₂, SO₂, NOₓ, particulates and VOCs.
  • Explain how sulfur and nitrogen oxides contribute to acid deposition.
  • Explain eutrophication and the connection between nutrient loading and dissolved oxygen.
  • Understand BOD as an indicator of oxygen consumption associated with biodegradable organic matter.
  • Know the major stages of water treatment and their purposes.
  • Identify important greenhouse gases and explain the greenhouse effect.
  • Distinguish stratospheric ozone from ground-level ozone.
  • Understand the simplified chemistry of ozone formation and catalytic ozone destruction.
  • Connect environmental chemistry to reaction rates, equilibrium, oxidation-reduction and atmospheric reactions.
High-value exam idea: Environmental chemistry is not just memorising pollutants. Be able to connect a pollutant to its source, chemical reaction, environmental effect and possible control method.
