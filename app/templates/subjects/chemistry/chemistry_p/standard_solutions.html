01

What Is a Primary Standard?

A primary standard is a reagent that is extremely pure, stable, has a high molar mass, and is not hygroscopic (does not absorb moisture from the air). This allows chemists to prepare a solution of known concentration directly by weighing.

Good Primary Standards

Anhydrous sodium carbonate (Na2CO3), Oxalic acid crystals (H2C2O4.2H2O), and Potassium hydrogen phthalate (KHP).

Not Primary Standards

Sodium hydroxide (NaOH) and Hydrochloric acid (HCl). NaOH absorbs moisture and CO2 rapidly, so its concentration must be standardized via titration.

02

Step-by-Step Laboratory Preparation

  1. Weighing accurately: Weigh an empty clean watch glass, add the solid primary standard, and record the precise mass difference on an analytical balance (to 2 or 3 decimal places).
  2. Dissolving: Transfer the solid into a clean beaker, add about 100 cm3 of distilled water, and stir with a glass rod until completely dissolved.
  3. Transferring: Pour the solution carefully through a filter funnel into a 250 cm3 volumetric flask. Rinse the beaker and funnel several times with distilled water, adding washings into the flask.
  4. Meneiskus Calibration: Add distilled water drop by drop using a dropping pipette until the bottom of the liquid meniscus rests exactly on the graduation mark at eye level.
  5. Homogenizing: Stopper the volumetric flask tightly and invert it thoroughly at least 5 times to ensure complete mixing.

04

Concentration Calculation Formula

Key Equations:
Moles = Mass / Molar Mass (n = m / M)
Concentration = Moles / Volume in Litres (C = n / V)

Worked Example: Na2CO3 Solution

Mass of Na2CO3 weighed = 2.65 g
Molar mass of Na2CO3 = (2 × 23.0) + 12.0 + (3 × 16.0) = 106.0 g/mol
Moles = 2.65 g / 106.0 g/mol = 0.0250 mol
Volume of volumetric flask = 250 cm3 (0.250 L)
Concentration = 0.0250 mol / 0.250 L = 0.100 mol/L (M)
