Functional Group Diagnostics

Identify and differentiate aldehydes, ketones, and carboxylic acids using Tollens' reagent, Fehling's solution, and sodium carbonate.

Aim Principle Key Tests Procedure Summary Table Identify Precautions Questions
01

Aim of the Experiment

To distinguish between carbonyl compounds (aldehydes and ketones) and carboxylic acids using selective chemical oxidation and acid-base characterization tests.

Key idea:
Aldehydes possess an easily oxidizable hydrogen atom attached to the carbonyl carbon, allowing them to act as reducing agents. Ketones resist mild oxidation, and carboxylic acids exhibit acidic properties with carbonates.
02

Principle of Diagnostics

Different carbonyl and carboxyl functionalities respond distinctively to specific reagents:

  • Aldehydes: Readily oxidized to carboxylic acids by mild oxidizing agents like Tollens' and Fehling's reagents.
  • Ketones: Lack the hydrogen atom required for mild oxidation, yielding negative results with Tollens' and Fehling's tests.
  • Carboxylic Acids: Contain acidic protons that react with weak bases like sodium carbonate or sodium bicarbonate to release carbon dioxide gas.
03

Key Reagents & Observations

1. Tollens' Test (Silver Mirror Test)

Tollens' reagent contains ammoniacal silver nitrate [Ag(NH3)2]+. When warmed with an aldehyde, silver ions are reduced to metallic silver, depositing a shiny silver mirror on the inner walls of the test tube. Ketones show no reaction.

2. Fehling's Test

Fehling's solution contains alkaline copper(II) complexes. When heated with an aldehyde, the blue Cu2+ ions are reduced to a brick-red precipitate of copper(I) oxide (Cu2O). Ketones do not react.

3. Sodium Carbonate Test

When a carboxylic acid is treated with aqueous sodium carbonate (Na2CO3) or sodium bicarbonate (NaHCO3), an acid-base reaction produces effervescence (bubbles) of carbon dioxide gas (CO2). Neither aldehydes nor ketones react.

04

Experimental Procedure

  1. Place 1 mL of each test compound (aldehyde, ketone, carboxylic acid) into separate clean test tubes.
  2. Tollens' Test: Add freshly prepared Tollens' reagent and warm gently in a water bath; check for a silver mirror.
  3. Fehling's Test: Add equal volumes of Fehling's A and B solutions, heat in a water bath, and check for a red precipitate.
  4. Carbonate Test: Add a pinch of solid sodium carbonate or a few drops of sodium carbonate solution and check for effervescence.

07

Precautions & Safety

  • Never store Tollens' reagent as it can form explosive silver fulminate compounds over time; prepare it fresh and dispose of it immediately after use.
  • Use water baths for heating flammable organic liquids rather than direct open flames.
  • Wear safety goggles and gloves when handling corrosive acids and reactive reagents.
08

Common Practical Questions

1. Why do aldehydes give a positive Tollens' test while ketones do not?
Aldehydes possess a hydrogen atom attached directly to the carbonyl carbon which makes them easily oxidizable, whereas ketones lack this hydrogen.
2. How do you confirm the presence of a carboxylic acid using a simple chemical test?
By adding sodium carbonate or sodium bicarbonate, which produces bubbling (effervescence) due to the release of carbon dioxide gas.
