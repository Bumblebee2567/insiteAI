Filtration & Washing

Separate suspended insoluble solids from liquids using gravity filtration or vacuum filtration, then wash the collected solid to remove soluble impurities.

Aim Principle Apparatus Gravity Filtration Vacuum Filtration Washing Compare Identify Precautions Questions
01

Aim of the Experiment

To separate an insoluble solid from a liquid using filtration and, where necessary, wash the collected solid to remove soluble impurities.

Key idea:
Filtration separates substances based on differences in particle size and physical state. The insoluble solid is retained by the filter while the liquid passes through.
02

Principle of Filtration

Filtration is a physical separation technique used when an insoluble solid is suspended in a liquid. A porous filter allows the liquid and dissolved substances to pass through while retaining the larger solid particles.

The liquid that passes through the filter is called the filtrate, while the solid retained on the filter paper is called the residue.

Gravity Filtration

Gravity pulls the liquid through filter paper placed inside a funnel. It is simple and useful when the filtrate is the main product.

Vacuum Filtration

Reduced pressure draws the liquid through the filter more quickly. It is particularly useful when the solid residue is the desired product.

Two important terms:
Residue = solid retained by the filter.
Filtrate = liquid that passes through the filter.
03

Apparatus & Materials

  • Filter funnel
  • Filter paper
  • Beaker or conical flask
  • Glass rod
  • Retort stand and clamp where required
  • Mixture containing an insoluble solid
  • Wash bottle containing a suitable washing liquid

Vacuum Filtration Equipment

  • Büchner funnel
  • Filter paper
  • Side-arm filter flask
  • Vacuum source
  • Suitable tubing
Important:
The filter paper must fit correctly inside the funnel. Poorly fitted filter paper can allow solid particles to pass through or cause leakage around the edges.
04

Gravity Filtration

Gravity filtration uses atmospheric pressure and gravity to pull the liquid through the filter paper.

Filter funnel
Collecting flask

Procedure

  1. Fold the filter paper to form a suitable cone and place it inside the funnel.
  2. Moisten the filter paper with a small amount of the appropriate liquid so that it fits closely against the funnel.
  3. Place the funnel securely above the receiving container.
  4. Pour the mixture slowly down a glass rod into the funnel.
  5. Keep the liquid level below the top edge of the filter paper.
  6. Allow the liquid to pass through completely.
  7. The solid remaining on the filter paper is the residue.
Why use a glass rod?
Pouring the mixture down a glass rod helps control the flow and reduces splashing or damage to the filter paper.
05

Vacuum Filtration

Vacuum filtration uses reduced pressure to draw the liquid through the filter. This is generally faster than ordinary gravity filtration.

Procedure

  1. Place suitable filter paper onto the Büchner funnel.
  2. Wet the filter paper with a small amount of the appropriate liquid so that it seals against the funnel.
  3. Assemble the Büchner funnel and filter flask securely.
  4. Connect the flask to the vacuum source using suitable tubing.
  5. Start the vacuum and carefully transfer the mixture onto the filter.
  6. Allow the liquid to be drawn through while the solid remains on the filter.
  7. Wash the collected solid if required.
Why use vacuum filtration?
It removes liquid from the solid more quickly and can leave the residue relatively dry compared with ordinary gravity filtration.
06

Washing the Residue

After filtration, the solid residue may still contain dissolved impurities from the original solution. Washing the residue helps remove these soluble substances.

Washing Procedure

  1. Allow most of the original liquid to pass through the filter.
  2. Add a small amount of a suitable washing liquid to the residue.
  3. Spread the washing liquid over the residue so that it contacts the solid thoroughly.
  4. Allow the washing liquid to pass through the filter.
  5. Repeat with small portions if further washing is needed.
  6. Continue filtration until the desired degree of washing has been achieved.
Why use small portions?
Several small portions of washing liquid can remove soluble impurities efficiently while avoiding unnecessary loss of the desired solid.

Choosing the Washing Liquid

The washing liquid should dissolve the unwanted soluble impurities but should not significantly dissolve or react with the desired solid.

07

If the question emphasizes collecting a solid quickly and removing as much liquid as possible, vacuum filtration is often the appropriate method.

09

Precautions & Sources of Error

Precautions

  • Use filter paper of the correct size and grade.
  • Do not overfill the filter paper.
  • Pour the mixture carefully to avoid tearing the filter paper.
  • Wet the filter paper before filtration when appropriate.
  • Use small portions of washing liquid.
  • Ensure vacuum filtration equipment is assembled securely.

Possible Sources of Error

  • Solid particles passing through damaged filter paper.
  • Pouring the mixture too quickly.
  • Using an incorrectly fitted filter paper.
  • Losing solid during transfer or washing.
  • Incomplete washing of the residue.
  • Failure to create an adequate vacuum.
Important:
Washing improves the purity of the residue, but excessive washing can cause some soluble products to be lost.
10

Common Practical Questions

1. What is filtration?
Filtration is a separation technique used to separate an insoluble solid from a liquid.
2. What is the residue?
The residue is the insoluble solid retained by the filter paper.
3. What is the filtrate?
The filtrate is the liquid that passes through the filter paper.
4. Why is the residue washed?
To remove soluble impurities that remain with the solid after filtration.
5. Why is vacuum filtration faster than gravity filtration?
Reduced pressure creates a pressure difference that draws the liquid through the filter more quickly.
6. Which method is usually preferred when the solid is the desired product?
Vacuum filtration is often preferred because it is faster and can produce a relatively drier solid.
7. Why should the mixture not be poured too quickly?
Pouring too quickly can overflow or damage the filter paper and may cause solid particles to pass through.
8. Why should small portions of washing liquid be used?
Small portions can remove soluble impurities effectively while reducing unnecessary loss of the desired solid.
9. What apparatus is commonly used for vacuum filtration?
A Büchner funnel, filter paper, side-arm filter flask, tubing, and a suitable vacuum source.
10. What is the main difference between filtration and evaporation?
Filtration separates an insoluble solid from a liquid, while evaporation removes a solvent to leave dissolved substances behind.
