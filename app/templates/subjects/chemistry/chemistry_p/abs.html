Acid–Base Practical (Acid into Base)

Study acid–base reactions, indicators, neutralisation and volumetric analysis by dripping acid from the burette into 25 cm3 of base in the flask.

Theory Indicators Apparatus Titration Results Calculation Practical Notes
01

Acids, Bases and Neutralisation

Acids produce hydrogen ions in aqueous solution, while bases react with acids and can accept hydrogen ions. When an acid reacts with a base, neutralisation occurs.

Acid

Hydrochloric acid is a strong acid delivered from the burette as the titrant.

Base

Sodium hydroxide is a strong alkali measured precisely at 25 cm3 in the conical flask.

Neutralisation: hydrochloric acid reacts with sodium hydroxide to produce sodium chloride and water.
HCl + NaOH → NaCl + H2O
02

Indicators

An indicator changes colour over a particular pH range and is used to identify the endpoint of an acid–base titration.

Methyl Orange (Yellow to Red)

In alkaline solution (25 cm3 NaOH in flask), methyl orange is yellow. As hydrochloric acid is added from the burette, the solution turns through orange and becomes red in acidic conditions.

Phenolphthalein

Phenolphthalein is pink in alkaline solution and becomes colourless when neutralised by acid.

For this practical: methyl orange is selected, so the visible transition is yellow → orange → red as acid is added to the alkali.
03

Titration Apparatus

Burette

Holds the hydrochloric acid and allows its volume to be measured accurately as it is delivered.

Conical Flask

Contains exactly 25 cm3 of sodium hydroxide base and methyl orange indicator.

Pipette

Used to measure out the fixed 25 cm3 volume of base safely into the flask.

06

Titration Calculation

For the reaction HCl + NaOH → NaCl + H2O, hydrochloric acid and sodium hydroxide react in a 1:1 mole ratio. Base volume = 25 cm3.

General mole relationship:
CaVa / na = CbVb / nb

Example calculation

Mean accurate titre of acid = 24.70 mL
Volume of base in flask = 25.00 mL (25 cm3)
Concentration of base = 0.100 mol/L
Moles of base = C × V = 0.100 × 25.00 / 1000 = 0.00250 mol
Since NaOH : HCl = 1 : 1, moles of HCl = 0.00250 mol
Concentration of acid = moles / volume = 0.00250 / 0.02470 = 0.101 mol/L
07

Practical Procedure

  1. Rinse the burette with the acid solution and fill it with hydrochloric acid.
  2. Record the initial burette reading.
  3. Use a pipette and pipette filler to transfer exactly 25 cm3 of sodium hydroxide into a clean conical flask.
  4. Add a few drops of methyl orange indicator to the base. The flask turns yellow.
  5. Place the flask directly below the burette tip on a white tile.
  6. Open the burette tap and add hydrochloric acid while swirling the flask continuously.
  7. As the endpoint approaches, add the acid slowly, drop by drop.
  8. With methyl orange, the colour changes from yellow through orange and then red as the solution becomes acidic.
  9. Record the final burette reading at the sharp orange endpoint.
  10. Calculate the titre and repeat until concordant results are obtained.
