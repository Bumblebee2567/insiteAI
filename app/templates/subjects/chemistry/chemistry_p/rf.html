Chromatography & R_f Values

Evaluate retention factors (R_f) of compounds separated along solid stationary phases using mobile chemical solvents.

Aim Principle Apparatus Procedure R_f Formula Calculate Precautions Questions
01

Aim of the Experiment

To separate mixtures of colored compounds (such as inks or plant pigments) using paper or thin-layer chromatography and calculate their respective retention factor (R_f) values for identification.

Key idea:
Components with a higher affinity for the mobile phase travel further up the plate, while those with a higher affinity for the stationary phase lag behind.
02

Principle of Chromatography

Chromatography relies on the distribution of solutes between two phases:

  • Stationary Phase: A fixed solid or liquid (e.g., chromatography paper or silica gel plate).
  • Mobile Phase: A flowing liquid solvent or solvent mixture that moves up the stationary phase via capillary action.
Why do spots separate?
Different substances dissolve differently in the solvent and adsorb differently to the paper, causing them to move at different rates and form distinct separated spots.
03

Apparatus & Materials

  • Chromatography paper or TLC strip
  • Beaker or developing chamber with a lid
  • Suitable solvent (e.g., ethanol, water, or acetone)
  • Capillary tube or spotter
  • Test mixture / sample solutions
  • Ruler (measured in millimeters or centimeters)
  • Pencil
Crucial Rule:
Always draw the baseline in pencil, never ink. Ink contains dyes that will dissolve in the solvent and ruin the chromatogram!
04

Experimental Procedure

  1. Draw a horizontal baseline in pencil near the bottom of the chromatography paper.
  2. Place a small spot of the mixture onto the baseline using a clean capillary tube.
  3. Pour a shallow layer of solvent into the beaker (ensure the solvent level sits below the pencil baseline).
  4. Lower the paper into the beaker, cover with a lid to maintain a saturated vapor environment, and let the solvent rise.
  5. Remove the paper just before the solvent front reaches the top, and immediately mark the solvent front position with a pencil.
  6. Allow the chromatogram to dry and calculate the R_f values.
05

Retention Factor (R_f) Formula

The retention factor (R_f) is a ratio used to identify compounds in chromatography. It is always constant for a given compound under identical conditions and has no units.

R_f = \frac{\text{Distance travelled by the substance}}{\text{Distance travelled by the solvent front}}

Note: R_f values are always between 0 and 1.

07

Precautions & Sources of Error

Precautions

  • Draw the baseline with a pencil, never ink.
  • Ensure the starting solvent level is below the baseline so samples do not dissolve directly into the pool.
  • Keep the beaker covered during development to prevent solvent evaporation.

Sources of Error

  • Spot applied too large, causing overlapping trails.
  • Solvent front allowed to run off the top of the paper.
  • Measuring distances from the top or middle of the spot instead of its center.
08

Common Practical Questions

1. Why must the baseline be drawn in pencil?
Pencil lead (graphite) is insoluble in solvents, whereas ink would dissolve and interfere with the results.
2. Why must the solvent level be below the baseline?
To prevent the sample spots from dissolving directly into the bulk solvent liquid instead of traveling up the paper.
3. Does an R_f value ever have units?
No, because it is a ratio of two distances (centimeters divided by centimeters), the units cancel out.
