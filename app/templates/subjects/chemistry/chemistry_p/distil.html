Distillation Columns

Separate mixtures of volatile organic liquids based on different boiling points using simple and fractional distillation setups.

Aim Principle Apparatus Simple Fractional Comparison Procedure Test Precautions Questions

1 Aim of Experiment

To separate mixtures of volatile liquids by exploiting differences in their boiling points using simple or fractional distillation.

Key idea: Distillation involves vaporising a liquid and then condensing the vapour back into a liquid. Components with different boiling points can therefore be separated.

2 Principle of Distillation

When a liquid mixture is heated, the component with the lower boiling point generally becomes richer in the vapour phase. The vapour is passed into a condenser where it is cooled and collected as a liquid.

The effectiveness of the separation depends on the difference between the boiling points of the components and the type of distillation used.

Boiling point: The temperature at which the vapour pressure of a liquid equals the surrounding pressure.

3 Apparatus & Materials

Distillation Flask

Holds the liquid mixture being heated.

Thermometer

Measures the temperature of the vapour entering the condenser.

Fractionating Column

Used in fractional distillation to provide repeated vaporisation and condensation.

Condenser

Cools vapour and converts it back into liquid.

Receiving Flask

Collects the condensed distillate.

Heat Source

Provides controlled heating of the mixture.

4 Simple Distillation

Simple distillation is commonly used when separating a volatile liquid from a non-volatile substance, or when the boiling points of two liquids are sufficiently far apart.

When to Use It

  • Separating a liquid from dissolved non-volatile substances.
  • Separating liquids with a relatively large boiling-point difference.
  • Obtaining a purified volatile liquid.

Main Features

  • No fractionating column is normally required.
  • The vapour passes directly to the condenser.
  • The distillate is collected after condensation.
Example: Simple distillation can be used to separate water from dissolved salts because the salt is non-volatile.

5 Fractional Distillation

Fractional distillation is used to separate miscible liquids whose boiling points are relatively close together.

A fractionating column is placed between the distillation flask and condenser. Inside the column, vapour repeatedly condenses and revaporises, improving the separation of the components.

Example: A mixture containing two miscible liquids boiling at about 78°C and 100°C can be separated using fractional distillation.

Fractionating Column

The column provides a large surface area where repeated condensation and vaporisation can occur.

Better Separation

The repeated cycles make the vapour reaching the top of the column richer in the more volatile component.

6 Simple vs Fractional Distillation

Feature Simple Distillation Fractional Distillation
Fractionating column Usually not required Required
Boiling-point difference Usually larger Relatively small
Repeated vaporisation/condensation Limited Occurs within the column
Typical application Liquid + non-volatile solute Miscible liquids

7 Experimental Procedure

1

Place the liquid mixture into the distillation flask, leaving sufficient space for boiling.

2

Add suitable anti-bumping granules where appropriate before heating.

3

Assemble the apparatus securely. For fractional distillation, place the fractionating column between the flask and condenser.

4

Position the thermometer bulb correctly near the entrance to the condenser so it measures the vapour temperature.

5

Connect the condenser cooling water so that water enters through the lower inlet and leaves through the upper outlet.

6

Heat the mixture gently and steadily while monitoring the temperature.

7

Collect the condensed liquid in the receiving flask and observe the temperature range during collection.

9 Precautions & Sources of Error

  • Make sure the apparatus is securely connected before heating.
  • Never heat a completely closed distillation system.
  • Position the thermometer bulb correctly so it measures the vapour temperature.
  • Heat gently to prevent violent boiling and carry-over of liquid.
  • Ensure cooling water is flowing through the condenser.
  • Do not distil to dryness unless the experimental procedure specifically requires it and it is safe to do so.
  • Avoid excessive heating because it can reduce the quality of the separation.
Common source of error: Incorrect thermometer placement can give an inaccurate temperature reading and make it difficult to identify the correct fraction.

10 Common Practical Questions

Why is fractional distillation used?
It improves separation of miscible liquids with relatively close boiling points.
What is the function of the fractionating column?
It allows repeated condensation and vaporisation, improving separation between components.
Why is the thermometer bulb placed near the condenser entrance?
So that it measures the temperature of the vapour entering the condenser.
Why does water enter the condenser from the bottom?
This keeps the condenser jacket filled with cooling water and provides effective cooling.
What is the distillate?
The liquid collected after the vapour has been condensed.
What happens if the mixture is heated too strongly?
Rapid boiling can cause liquid to be carried over and can reduce the effectiveness of the separation.
