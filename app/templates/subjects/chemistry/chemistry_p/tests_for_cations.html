Analytical Chemistry Principles

Metal cations in aqueous solution form distinct, characteristic precipitates when reacted with hydroxide ions (OH-) from Sodium Hydroxide (NaOH) or Aqueous Ammonia (NH3).

1. Adding a Few Drops

Initially, transition metals and post-transition metals precipitate out of solution as insoluble metal hydroxides.

2. Adding in Excess

Certain precipitates will re-dissolve by reacting further to form soluble complex ions or by showing amphoteric behavior.

Precipitation & Complexing Equations

Understanding the underlying net ionic equations is critical for matching lab results to dry paper theoretical questions.

Standard Precipitation:
Cu2+(aq) + 2OH-(aq) → Cu(OH)2(s) (Pale blue precipitate)
Amphoteric Dissolution (Aluminium in Excess NaOH):
Al(OH)3(s) + OH-(aq) → [Al(OH)4]-(aq) (Soluble, Colorless Tetrahydroxoaluminate Complex)
Ligand Exchange / Complexation (Copper in Excess NH3):
Cu(OH)2(s) + 4NH3(aq) → [Cu(NH3)4]2+(aq) + 2OH-(aq) (Soluble, Deep Blue Tetraamminediaquacopper Complex)
