The pH Scale

The pH scale runs from 0 to 14 and measures how acidic or alkaline (basic) an aqueous solution is. It is based on the concentration of hydrogen ions in the solution.

Acidic (pH 0 to 6.9)

Solutions with a high concentration of hydrogen ions. Lower pH numbers indicate stronger acidity.

Neutral (pH 7.0)

Pure water at room temperature. The concentration of hydrogen ions equals hydroxide ions.

Alkaline / Basic (pH 7.1 to 14)

Solutions containing excess hydroxide ions. Higher pH numbers indicate stronger alkalinity.

Key formula: pH = -log10[H+]. Every whole unit change on the pH scale represents a tenfold change in hydrogen ion concentration.

Common Organic Indicators

Indicators are weak organic acids or bases whose ionized and unionized forms have distinct colors. Selecting the right indicator depends on the pH at the equivalence point of a titration.

Universal Indicator & pH Paper

Unlike single indicators that only show a two-color change, Universal Indicator is a blend of several compounds that exhibits a smooth spectrum of colors from pH 1 to 14.

Strong Acids (pH 1–3)

Turns deep red or pink. Indicates high concentration of hydrogen ions.

Neutral (pH 7)

Turns distinct green. Found in pure water and neutral salt solutions.

Strong Alkalis (pH 12–14)

Turns dark purple or violet. Indicates high concentration of hydroxide ions.

05

Practical Laboratory Procedure

  1. Pour approximately 2 cm3 of the test solution into a clean test tube.
  2. Add 1 to 2 drops of the chosen indicator using a dropping pipette.
  3. Swirl the test tube gently to mix the indicator thoroughly into the solution.
  4. Observe the resulting color against a white background or tile for clarity.
  5. Compare the observed color with a standard colorimetric pH chart to determine the approximate pH.
  6. Repeat the process using fresh test tubes and different indicators for accuracy.
Safety Tip: Always use dropping pipettes carefully to avoid chemical splashes, and wear safety goggles when handling concentrated acids or alkalis.
