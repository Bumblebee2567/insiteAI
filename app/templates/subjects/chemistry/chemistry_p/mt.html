01

Aim of the Module

To understand how to record raw experimental data correctly, determine instrument uncertainties, calculate percentage errors, and propagate errors through multi-step calculations.

Key idea:
Every physical measurement has a degree of inherent uncertainty determined by the precision of the measuring instrument.
02

Principles of Error Analysis

Experimental errors can be broadly classified into two categories:

  • Random Errors: Unpredictable variations in measurements caused by factors like parallax or environmental fluctuations. Minimized by taking multiple readings and calculating averages.
  • Systematic Errors: Consistent, repeatable errors stemming from faulty calibration or incorrect experimental design (e.g., a balance not zeroed properly).
  • Percentage Error Formula: Percentage Error = (Absolute Uncertainty / Measured Value) × 100%.
03

Instrument Tolerance & Uncertainty

The uncertainty of an analogue instrument is typically half of the smallest graduation mark (± half-division), while digital instruments have an uncertainty equal to ± 1 unit of the last displayed decimal place.

Example - Burette Readings:
A standard burette is graduated in 0.1 cm3 intervals. Because you record both an initial and final reading (two measurements combined), the total absolute uncertainty is ± 0.05 + ± 0.05 = ± 0.10 cm3.
04

Data Recording Best Practices

  1. Always record all raw data to the consistent number of decimal places matching the precision of the instrument.
  2. Ensure all table columns include clear headings with proper SI units (e.g., Mass / g, Volume / cm3).
  3. Perform repeat titrations or measurements until concordant values are achieved.
  4. Calculate percentage uncertainties for every piece of equipment used in a derived result.

07

Precautions & Sources of Error

  • Avoid parallax errors by ensuring your eye level is perpendicular to the meniscus when reading burettes or volumetric flasks.
  • Ensure electronic balances are placed on stable, vibration-free surfaces and tared to zero before weighing.
  • Allow heated apparatus to cool down to room temperature before taking mass measurements to prevent convection currents from affecting the balance.
08

Common Practical Questions

1. Why is the total uncertainty of a burette titre calculated using two individual readings?
A titre value is determined by taking both an initial burette reading and a final burette reading, meaning the absolute uncertainty of the instrument applies to each reading.
2. What is the difference between random errors and systematic errors?
Random errors cause scatter around the true value due to unpredictable fluctuations and can be reduced by averaging repeats. Systematic errors shift all measurements consistently in one direction due to calibration issues or faulty equipment.
