Catalog Overview

Proper selection and handling of laboratory equipment are essential for accurate experimentation, safety, and reproducible results in chemistry.

Key idea:
Different equipment is engineered for specific tolerances, thermal thresholds, and measurement sensitivities.
02

Common Laboratory Glassware

Most high-grade laboratory glassware is made from borosilicate glass (such as Pyrex) due to its low thermal expansion coefficient and high resistance to chemical corrosion.

  • Beakers & Conical Flasks: Used for general mixing, holding, and heating liquids where high volumetric precision is not required.
  • Volumetric Flasks: Calibrated to contain a single exact volume at 20°C for making standard solutions.
  • Burettes & Pipettes: Precision measuring glassware designed to deliver variable and fixed volumes during titrations.
03

Heating Elements & Thermal Equipment

Thermal energy input requires controlled equipment depending on the boiling point and flammability of the chemicals:

  • Bunsen Burners: Provide a direct open flame fueled by natural gas or LPG for high-temperature inorganic heating.
  • Heating Mantles: Fitted with insulated electrical heating elements designed to snugly fit round-bottom flasks, ideal for flammable organic refluxes.
  • Water Baths & Hot Plates: Provide uniform, indirect heating to prevent local overheating or ignition of volatile organic solvents.
04

Vacuum Sensors & Filtration Systems

Vacuum systems are critical for rapid suction filtration, reduced-pressure distillations, and rotary evaporation.

  • Büchner Funnels & Flasks: Heavy-walled glassware paired with vacuum suction for fast solid-liquid separation.
  • Manometers & Pirani Gauges: Vacuum sensors used to monitor system pressure during vacuum distillation setups.
  • Water Aspirators & Diaphragm Pumps: Mechanical devices utilized to pull negative pressure across closed systems.

Equipment Care & Maintenance

  • Always clean glassware thoroughly with distilled water and appropriate solvents after use.
  • Never place hot crucibles or objects directly onto analytical balance pans.
  • Inspect vacuum glassware for star-cracks or chips before applying reduced pressure to prevent implosion.
08

Common Practical Questions

1. Why is borosilicate glass preferred over standard soda-lime glass for heating applications?
Borosilicate glass has a very low coefficient of thermal expansion, preventing it from cracking or shattering under rapid thermal shocks.
2. What is the difference between glassware calibrated to "contain" (TC) versus glassware calibrated to "deliver" (TD)?
TC glassware (like volumetric flasks) holds an exact volume when filled to the calibration mark. TD glassware (like burettes and pipettes) is calibrated to deliver that exact volume accounting for residual liquid clinging to the inner walls.
