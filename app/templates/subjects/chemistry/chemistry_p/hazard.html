Safety & Chemical Hazards

Implement proper personal protective equipment (PPE), ventilation protocols, and chemical waste disposal standards in the laboratory.

Aim Personal Protection Ventilation Protocols Waste Disposal Hazard Symbols Identify Emergency Actions Questions
01

Aim of Safety Protocols

To establish a secure working environment by recognizing chemical hazards, correctly utilizing personal protective gear, ensuring adequate ventilation, and adhering to strict waste disposal regulations.

Key idea:
Safety in the laboratory is paramount. Every chemical reagent carries potential hazards that must be respected through proper handling procedures.
02

Personal Protective Equipment (PPE)

Standard laboratory attire is designed to shield skin, eyes, and clothing from accidental splashes or spills:

  • Safety Goggles: Must be worn at all times during practical work to protect eyes from corrosive splashes or flying glass.
  • Laboratory Coats: Buttoned-up cotton or poly-cotton coats protect skin and prevent chemicals from soaking into personal clothing.
  • Gloves: Nitrile or neoprene gloves should be selected based on chemical compatibility when handling toxic or corrosive substances.
  • Footwear: Fully enclosed shoes covering the entire foot are mandatory (no sandals or open-toe shoes).
03

Ventilation & Fume Hood Protocols

Inhalation of toxic vapors, corrosive gases, or volatile organic solvents must be strictly prevented:

  • Fume Hoods: Always perform reactions involving hazardous gases (like Cl2, SO2, or volatile organic liquids) inside a certified fume hood.
  • Sash Height: Keep the fume hood sash at or below the recommended operating level to maintain proper air velocity and face suction.
  • Clutter Control: Avoid storing chemical containers or equipment long-term inside fume hoods, as this disrupts airflow patterns.
04

Chemical & Solid Waste Disposal

Never dispose of hazardous chemical waste down standard sinks unless explicitly instructed by laboratory supervisors:

  • Organic Solvents: Collected in dedicated non-halogenated or halogenated organic waste carboys.
  • Heavy Metal Solutions: Precipitation or collection in designated inorganic heavy metal waste containers (e.g., copper, silver, or lead residues).
  • Sharps & Glassware: Broken glass and needles must go into rigid, puncture-resistant sharps containers rather than standard trash bins.
05

Standard Hazard Pictograms

Hazard Category Description Precautions
Corrosive Causes severe skin burns and eye damage (e.g., concentrated acids/bases). Wear face shield, gloves, and apron; handle in fume hood.
Flammable Ignites easily upon exposure to sparks, heat, or open flames. Keep away from open flames; use heating mantles or water baths.
Toxic / Acute Fatal or harmful if swallowed, inhaled, or absorbed through skin. Work in fume hood, wear respirator if necessary, avoid skin contact.

Emergency Actions & First Aid

  • Eye Contact: Immediately flush eyes at the eyewash station with a continuous stream of water for at least 15 minutes. Seek medical attention.
  • Chemical Spills on Skin: Rinse thoroughly under a safety shower or tap water. Remove contaminated clothing immediately.
  • Fire Emergencies: Use fire blankets for small clothing fires, or appropriate fire extinguishers for solvent fires. Evacuate if the fire cannot be safely contained.
08

Common Practical Questions

1. Why is it dangerous to dispose of organic solvents down standard laboratory sinks?
Organic solvents can release flammable vapors into plumbing systems, cause environmental contamination, and damage municipal drainage pipes.
2. What immediate action should be taken if concentrated acid is spilled on your skin?
Immediately flush the affected skin area with copious amounts of running water for at least 10 to 15 minutes and inform the laboratory instructor.
