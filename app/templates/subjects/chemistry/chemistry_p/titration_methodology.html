Titration Methodology

Master precise standard volumetric titrations using burettes, pipettes, and stoichiometric indicators to achieve concordant results.

Apparatus Handling Rinsing Rules Procedure Minimizing Errors
01

Core Titration Apparatus

Volumetric titrations rely on precision glassware designed to measure and deliver specific volumes of liquids.

Burette

Delivers variable accurate volumes of titrant. Read the bottom of the meniscus at eye level to 0.05 cm3.

Pipette

Delivers a fixed, exact volume (usually 25.0 cm3) of analyte solution into the conical flask.

Conical Flask

Holds the analyte and indicator. Sloped sides prevent splashing when swirling the mixture.

02

Golden Rinsing Rules (Crucial for Exams)

Burette: Rinse with the solution to be placed inside it (titrant). If rinsed with water, any residual water dilutes the titrant, causing incorrect titration readings.
Pipette: Rinse with distilled water, then with the solution it will measure. Never rinse with anything else to avoid altering the moles of solute delivered.
Conical Flask: Rinse only with distilled water. Residual water does not change the total number of moles of reactant inside the flask!
03

Step-by-Step Titration Execution

  1. Fill the burette with titrant and open the tap briefly to fill the jet tip and remove air bubbles. Record the initial reading.
  2. Use a volumetric pipette and safety filler to transfer 25.0 cm3 of analyte solution into a clean conical flask.
  3. Add 2 to 3 drops of a suitable indicator (e.g., methyl orange) to the flask.
  4. Perform a rough titration first to quickly find the approximate endpoint volume.
  5. Perform subsequent accurate titrations, swirling the flask continuously with one hand while controlling the burette tap with the other. Add titrant drop by drop near the endpoint until the first permanent color change occurs.
  6. Record initial and final burette readings, calculate titres, and repeat until concordant results (within 0.10 cm3) are achieved.
