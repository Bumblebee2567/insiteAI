Redox Titrations

Carry out standard oxidation-reduction calculations using potassium manganate(VII) (KMnO4) as a self-indicating oxidizing agent.

Aim Principle Procedure Calculations Observations Identify Precautions Questions
01

Aim of the Experiment

To determine the concentration or purity of a reducing agent (such as iron(II) ions or oxalic acid) by performing a volumetric redox titration against standard potassium manganate(VII).

Key idea:
In a redox titration, electrons are transferred from the reducing agent to the oxidizing agent. Stoichiometric mole ratios are used to calculate unknown concentrations.
02

Principle of Manganate Titrations

Potassium manganate(VII) acts as a powerful oxidizing agent in acidic solutions:

  • Self-Indicator: Purple MnO4- ions are reduced to colorless Mn2+ ions. Because the reactant is intensely colored and the product is colorless, no external chemical indicator is required.
  • The Endpoint: The very first drop of excess KMnO4 added beyond the equivalence point turns the titration mixture a permanent pale pink color.
  • Acidification: Dilute sulfuric acid (H2SO4) is always added to provide the necessary hydrogen ions for the complete reduction of manganate. (Nitric or hydrochloric acids are avoided as they interfere with redox reactions).
03

Experimental Procedure

  1. Rinse and fill the burette with standard potassium manganate(VII) solution (KMnO4).
  2. Pipette a measured volume (e.g., 25.0 cm3) of the reducing agent solution (e.g., Fe2+) into a clean conical flask.
  3. Add an excess of dilute sulfuric acid to the conical flask to ensure acidic conditions.
  4. Titrate by running KMnO4 from the burette into the flask with swirling until a permanent pale pink color persists.
  5. Record the titre volume and repeat until concordant results (within 0.10 cm3) are achieved.
04

Redox Calculation Steps

To solve redox titration problems, follow these systematic steps:

  • Step 1: Write out the balanced half-equations and overall ionic equation for the redox reaction.
  • Step 2: Calculate the moles of the known substance using the formula: Moles = Concentration × Volume (in dm3).
  • Step 3: Use the stoichiometric mole ratio from the balanced equation to find the moles of the unknown substance.
  • Step 4: Determine the concentration or mass of the unknown in the desired volume or sample.

07

Precautions & Safety

  • Potassium manganate(VII) is an oxidizing agent and a skin irritant; wear gloves and eye protection.
  • Read the burette from the top of the meniscus (curved surface) for KMnO4 due to its intense purple color obscuring the bottom meniscus.
  • Ensure sulfuric acid is added in sufficient quantities; insufficient acid leads to brown MnO2 precipitation instead of colorless Mn2+.
08

Common Practical Questions

1. Why is dilute sulfuric acid used to acidify the titration mixture instead of hydrochloric acid?
HCl can be oxidized by KMnO4 to toxic chlorine gas, which would interfere with stoichiometry and titre volumes. Sulfuric acid is stable against oxidation under these conditions.
2. What color change marks the endpoint of a potassium manganate(VII) titration?
From colorless (or pale green) to a permanent pale pink.
