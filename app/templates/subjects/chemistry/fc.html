What Is a Functional Group?

A functional group is a specific arrangement of atoms within an organic molecule that gives the molecule its characteristic chemical behaviour. The carbon skeleton provides the framework, while the functional group strongly influences how the molecule reacts.

Key idea:
When two different organic molecules contain the same functional group, they often show related chemical behaviour because that group provides a common reactive site.

Alcohol

Contains a hydroxyl group attached to carbon.

R–OH

Carboxylic Acid

Contains a carbonyl group together with a hydroxyl group.

R–COOH

Ester

Contains a carbonyl group bonded to an oxygen that is connected to another carbon group.

R–COOR

Aldehyde

Contains a terminal carbonyl carbon bonded to hydrogen.

R–CHO

Ketone

Contains a carbonyl carbon bonded to two carbon groups.

R–CO–R

Amine

Contains nitrogen bonded into a hydrocarbon framework.

R–NH₂

03

Carboxylic Acids

Carboxylic acids contain the carboxyl group, –COOH. The group combines a carbonyl C=O with a hydroxyl –OH group.

R–COOH General structure of a carboxylic acid
Example Ethanoic acid
CH₃–COOH
Characteristic group Carboxyl
–C(=O)OH

Reaction with an alcohol

Carboxylic acid + Alcohol ⇌ Ester + Water
This reaction is commonly called esterification. It forms an ester and water from a carboxylic acid and an alcohol.
04

Esters

Esters contain the functional arrangement R–COOR. They can be formed when a carboxylic acid reacts with an alcohol.

R–C(=O)–O–R′ General structure of an ester
Example — Ethyl ethanoate
CH₃COOCH₂CH₃

Ethyl ethanoate is formed from ethanoic acid and ethanol.

CH₃COOH + CH₃CH₂OH ⇌ CH₃COOCH₂CH₃ + H₂O
Naming clue: Ester names commonly contain an alkyl part followed by an –oate ending, such as ethyl ethanoate.
05

Aldehydes

Aldehydes contain a carbonyl group in which the carbonyl carbon is bonded to at least one hydrogen atom.

R–CHO General structure of an aldehyde
Functional arrangement Terminal carbonyl
R–C(=O)–H
Example Ethanal
CH₃–CHO
Aldehydes are commonly identified by the –CHO group and use the –al naming ending.
06

Ketones

Ketones contain a carbonyl group in which the carbonyl carbon is bonded to two carbon groups.

R–CO–R′ General structure of a ketone
Functional arrangement Internal carbonyl
R–C(=O)–R′
Example Propanone
CH₃–CO–CH₃
Fast identification: If the carbonyl carbon has carbon groups on both sides, you are looking at a ketone.
07

Amines

Amines are nitrogen-containing organic compounds in which nitrogen is bonded to one or more carbon-containing groups. Simple amines include primary, secondary and tertiary forms.

Primary amine 1°
R–NH₂
Secondary amine 2°
R–NH–R′
Tertiary amine 3°
R–N(R′)–R″
Example Ethanamine
CH₃–CH₂–NH₂
Important: Amines are generally basic because the nitrogen atom has a lone pair that can participate in acid-base reactions.

Quick Identification Method

When given an unfamiliar organic structure, locate the important atoms and bonds first. The carbonyl group, C=O, is especially useful because what is attached to the carbonyl carbon determines whether the compound is an aldehyde, ketone, acid or ester.

Look for –OH
Look for C=O + H
Look for C=O + C
Look for –COOH
Look for –COOR
Look for –NH₂

Identify the functional group in CH₃COCH₃

Answer: CH₃COCH₃ is a ketone, specifically propanone.

Exam Checklist

  • Be able to identify the functional group from a structural or displayed formula.
  • Know the general structures of alcohols, aldehydes, ketones, carboxylic acids, esters and amines.
  • Distinguish an aldehyde from a ketone by examining what is attached to the carbonyl carbon.
  • Recognize that carboxylic acids contain both C=O and –OH within the same functional group.
  • Recognize esters as compounds containing R–COOR.
  • Know the major naming endings: –ol, –al, –one, –oic acid, –oate and –amine.
  • Understand the basic relationship between alcohols, carboxylic acids and esters.
