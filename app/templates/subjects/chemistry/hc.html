What Are Hydrocarbons?

Hydrocarbons are organic compounds containing only carbon and hydrogen atoms. Carbon's ability to form four covalent bonds allows hydrocarbons to form chains, branches and rings.

Key idea: Hydrocarbons can be classified according to the type of carbon-carbon bonding present in their molecules.

Single Bonds

Saturated hydrocarbons containing only C–C single bonds are called alkanes.

Double Bonds

Hydrocarbons containing C=C double bonds are called alkenes.

Triple Bonds

Hydrocarbons containing C≡C triple bonds are called alkynes.

Alkanes

Alkanes are saturated hydrocarbons because they contain only carbon-carbon single bonds.

CnH2n+2 General formula of open-chain alkanes

First Members of the Series

Name Formula Number of Carbon Atoms
Methane CH4 1
Ethane C2H6 2
Propane C3H8 3
Butane C4H10 4
Pentane C5H12 5

Major Reaction: Combustion

Alkanes undergo combustion in oxygen. Complete combustion produces carbon dioxide and water.

CH4 + 2O2 → CO2 + 2H2O
Incomplete combustion: When oxygen is limited, carbon monoxide and/or carbon may form instead of carbon dioxide.

Addition Reactions

The double bond makes alkenes more reactive than alkanes. Addition reactions occur across the C=C bond.

Hydrogenation

Ethene + Hydrogen

C2H4 + H2 → C2H6

Usually carried out using a nickel catalyst.

Bromination

Ethene + Bromine

C2H4 + Br2 → C2H4Br2

Bromine water is decolourised, providing a test for carbon-carbon double bonds.

Hydrocarbon Calculations

Example: Finding the Molecular Formula of an Alkane

An alkane contains 6 carbon atoms. Determine its molecular formula.

1
Use the alkane general formula: CnH2n+2
2
Substitute n = 6: C6H2(6)+2
3
Calculate the number of hydrogen atoms: 2(6) + 2 = 14
Answer: C6H14 — hexane.

Example: Complete Combustion

Balance the combustion equation for propane.

C3H8 + O2 → CO2 + H2O
1
Balance carbon: C3H8 → 3CO2
2
Balance hydrogen: 8 hydrogen atoms require 4H2O.
3
Count oxygen atoms on the right: 6 + 4 = 10 oxygen atoms.
4
Therefore 5O2 are required.
Balanced equation: C3H8 + 5O2 → 3CO2 + 4H2O

Physical Properties

Boiling Point

Boiling points generally increase as molecular size increases because intermolecular attractions become stronger.

Solubility

Hydrocarbons are generally non-polar and are poorly soluble in water.

Flammability

Many hydrocarbons burn readily in oxygen and release considerable energy.

Trend: As carbon-chain length increases, boiling point generally increases while volatility decreases.
